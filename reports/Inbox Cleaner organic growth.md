# Grow Gmail traffic through useful evidence

**Inbox Cleaner should repair its existing acquisition journey, then build useful cleanup tools and stronger intent-owner guides before publishing its remaining topic queue.** The audit found a workable static website with 50 live sitemap URLs and 47 guides, but extensive drift between the extension being promoted and the browser application described in those guides. More pages would currently repeat those inconsistencies. Unlimited coding bandwidth is best spent producing tested Gmail examples, safe query utilities, real product demonstrations, automatic editorial checks and a measurable installation journey. This can run on the current static hosting and free first-party reporting without a paid keyword platform, writers, backlinks or advertising. The initial priority is qualified desktop Gmail users who need sender discovery and multiselect cleanup; broader storage and subscription content should accurately explain Gmail's native options and the extension's limits. This report recommends a finite repair sprint, 15 bounded page/tool briefs and expansion decisions over 30, 60 and 90 days rather than a traffic forecast.

Audit date: **17 October 2026, Asia/Kolkata**. Evidence combines live HTTP checks, repository inspection, official Google documentation, public competitor research and the owner's Search Console inspection. “Confirmed” below identifies observed facts; proposed work and prioritization are recommendations. This phase produced research and recommendations, not website, extension, store or scheduler changes. The installed `seo-audit`, `content-strategy` and `programmatic-seo` workflows informed the audit; they are free local skills, not paid SEO services. ### The central conversion problem is inconsistent descriptions of the product

The complete article scan found **41 of 47 bodies mentioning Gmail API, 33 mentioning Connect Gmail, and zero inline images across all 47**. Yet the currently promoted extension uses Gmail interface automation and native selection/Trash controls. It discovers senders through category scans, obtains exact counts for at most the top 250 discovered senders, leaves others approximate and does not guarantee discovering every sender in the account. Source selectors are English-dependent, and a full scan can take minutes. Its sender deletion scope can reach archived mail, so a reader's expectation of “Inbox only” or “exclude starred” cannot silently carry into that action. These are source observations requiring hands-on confirmation against the published package before becoming final marketing promises. ([Inventory](</Users/pushpendrasingh/projects/inbox-cleaner/research_notes/Inbox Cleaner organic growth/content-inventory.csv>); [extension scanning and deletion](/Users/pushpendrasingh/projects/inbox-cleaner/content.js); [confirmation flow](/Users/pushpendrasingh/projects/inbox-cleaner/popup.js); [README](/Users/pushpendrasingh/projects/inbox-cleaner/README.md))

The homepage also argues that Gmail is limited to 50 emails per operation, while its own FAQ acknowledges the select-all-conversations link. Google explicitly documents selecting every matching conversation beyond the current page. The extension itself uses that native mechanism, so “bypasses Gmail selection,” “no timeouts” and similar article claims are unsupported for the extension. Replace the false limitation with a concrete benefit hypothesis: **see which discovered senders dominate Gmail, review them, then move selected senders' messages to Trash in desktop Chrome.** Demonstrate the convenience for multiple senders rather than pretending Gmail cannot bulk-delete. ([Homepage](https://inbox-cleaner.vercel.app/); [Google deletion instructions](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/7401?hl=en); [mass-delete article](https://inbox-cleaner.vercel.app/blog/how-to-mass-delete-emails-gmail.html); [native-selection implementation](/Users/pushpendrasingh/projects/inbox-cleaner/content.js:330))

The homepage mixes installation, a browser OAuth application, an embedded Connect Gmail tool and a Pro waitlist. The privacy page describes Gmail API/OAuth and says the only network calls are to Gmail API, while the homepage also loads Google services and a waitlist sends email addresses to Apps Script. Scope claims precisely: extension mail handling, browser OAuth handling, ordinary site requests and voluntary waitlist submissions are different flows. Make extension installation the primary journey and give the browser version a separately labeled route if it remains supported. Do not remove it or assert it is broken without testing and usage evidence. The live store listing showed **version 3.2.1, updated 16 October 2026, five users and no ratings**; this supersedes internal notes saying that version was unpublished. Five public store users is neither an active-user count nor organic install attribution. ([Privacy](https://inbox-cleaner.vercel.app/privacy.html); [homepage](https://inbox-cleaner.vercel.app/); [waitlist implementation](/Users/pushpendrasingh/projects/inbox-cleaner/docs/app.js:533); [live store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/inbox-cleaner/pnlnpdidhjimchppdmojledmfakpepep))

Two existing instructions deserve immediate correction because they can mislead users beyond ordinary marketing drift. The filter article presents `category:promotions older_than:30d` as an ongoing aging rule, although Gmail filters act on incoming mail rather than periodically revisiting old messages. The bank-statements article says items older than seven years are almost always safe to delete; official IRS guidance includes indefinite retention cases and warns other organizations can require longer retention. Replace that blanket assurance with scoped, sourced guidance or remove the financial-retention advice. Explicitly explain that the extension's sender-wide deletion does not inherit an article's manual `-is:starred` query. ([Filter article](https://inbox-cleaner.vercel.app/blog/gmail-filters-delete-emails-automatically.html); [Google filters help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6579?hl=en); [bank article](https://inbox-cleaner.vercel.app/blog/delete-bank-statement-emails-safely-gmail.html); [IRS record retention](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records))

### Use free measurement without inventing attribution

Start with Search Console and Chrome Web Store developer reporting. Export weekly page/query/device/country views, separate branded/nonbranded discovery and annotate every substantial content/product/domain change. Compare rolling 28-day windows where data permits, and inspect page/query cohorts rather than declaring success from a single day's clicks. Search Console tracks search exposure, not the whole user funnel. Chrome reports store acquisition/install data, but a website outbound click is not an installed or activated user, and article-level attribution must not be asserted without a supported implementation. ([Search Console introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start); [Chrome metrics](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/metrics))

| Measurement | Free initial source | Decision it supports / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Search impressions, clicks, CTR, position, queries | GSC Performance | Which jobs receive exposure; position is an aggregate diagnostic, not a fixed rank or causal proof. |
| Submitted/indexed pages, exclusions, canonicals | GSC sitemap/Page Indexing/URL Inspection | Which important URLs need diagnosis; indexed percentage alone is not success or a mandate to index every URL. |
| Organic landings and store CTA clicks | Existing hosting data if available; minimal privacy-consistent site analytics if implemented | Which articles bring relevant visits and interest; cannot equate a click with an install. |
| Store listing views, installs, uninstalls | Chrome developer dashboard | Listing conversion and acquisition trends; confirm available dimensions before promising attribution. |
| First scan, successful cleanup, recovery/support incidents | Voluntary anonymous feedback initially; separately designed aggregate measurement later | Product usefulness and failure burden; no silent extension telemetry conflicting with privacy promises. |
| Editorial defect count and verified coverage | Versioned facts/inventory/build report | Whether output quality survives increased publishing capacity. |

Website events must exclude email addresses, sender domains, mail content, message IDs and personal search query strings. Keep tool inputs local. Avoid introducing a paid analytics backend or an email-content collection pipeline to answer simple acquisition questions. Initial cash spending can remain zero on tools and current hosting; a domain is an optional small brand decision rather than an SEO prerequisite.

Begin with roughly two or three evidence-complete content/asset releases per week plus immediate factual repairs, then increase only as many as satisfy the quality contract. That is a workload proposal, not an SEO frequency rule. Unlimited coding should accelerate original evidence, testing and maintenance rather than force editorial output. The observed 13-click search baseline is too small for reliable conversion forecasts. No numeric growth or ROI target is defensible until store acquisition and actual task success can also guide decisions.

Free distribution should reuse these assets: a concise GitHub tools section, short native-workflow demonstrations, maintainable synthetic fixtures and genuinely useful answers to existing questions. Prepare targeted suggestions for relevant tutorial authors or communities; do not automate unsolicited posts or backlink placement. Chrome's own discovery guidance emphasizes useful onboarding, complete accurate listings and quality assets, so improving store presentation belongs alongside website work. ### What remains unknown

The public audit did not establish backlinks, installed-extension task success, mobile layout quality, keyboard accessibility, Lighthouse results, conversion rates or article-to-install attribution. No field Core Web Vitals data should be interpreted as a pass or failure. The homepage includes fonts, Identity Services and legacy app code, but byte counts alone do not establish a speed problem; measure mobile Lighthouse/PageSpeed and field evidence before prioritizing optimization. Competitor search samples show available formats, not reliable Google ranks, search volumes or traffic shares. Full first-party query exports and store dashboard data should refine the proposed priorities, and current product tests should decide which claims can safely survive.

## Conclusion

The most useful constraint is now an evidence budget: every additional indexable page creates a continuing obligation to keep Gmail instructions, product behavior, permissions and recovery accurate. Reusable fixtures, a product-fact registry and a draft-to-verified publishing system convert plentiful coding capacity into a defensible advantage. Without that system, increased output increases stale claims and overlapping journeys faster than it creates useful acquisition assets.

The next implementation should be a concrete repair-and-first-tool release with the acceptance criteria above. That release gives future content a truthful conversion destination and produces the measurement needed to decide where expansion deserves effort. Organic growth then becomes an iterative product-and-content discipline grounded in real user jobs rather than a race to exhaust the topic queue.
