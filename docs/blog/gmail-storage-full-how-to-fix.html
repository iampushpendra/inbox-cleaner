Gmail Storage Full? How to Fix It

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: Gmail storage full is fixed by checking what's using space at one.google.com/storage, searching Gmail for size:10m to find your largest emails, deleting the worst offenders, and then emptying Trash so the space releases immediately instead of after 30 days. If years of buildup across many senders is the real cause, Inbox Cleaner ranks senders by volume so you can clear the biggest sources in one click.

When Gmail's storage fills up, you stop receiving new email and can't send anything until you're back under the limit. It's disruptive precisely because it usually happens without warning — you get a bounce notice or a "storage full" banner, and now it's urgent instead of something you can put off.

The fix has two parts: get back under the limit right now, and figure out what actually filled it so it doesn't happen again in a few months.

Why is my Gmail storage suddenly full?

Gmail's free 15 GB isn't just email — it's shared across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos on the same Google account. A lot of people assume their inbox is the culprit when the real space is going to full-resolution photo backups or a handful of large video files sitting in Drive. Before you start deleting emails, it's worth confirming where the space is actually going.

How do I check what's using my Gmail storage?

  1. Go to one.google.com/storage while signed into the affected account.
  2. Google shows a breakdown by service — Gmail, Drive, and Photos each get a slice with their own size.
  3. If Gmail is the big slice, the fixes below apply directly. If Drive or Photos is bigger, clearing email won't move the needle much — you'll need to clean up files or photos instead.

How do I quickly free up Gmail storage when it's full?

If Gmail itself is the problem, attack it by size first, not by date — a handful of large attachments usually accounts for more space than thousands of plain-text notifications combined.

  1. In Gmail's search bar, type size:10m and press Enter. This surfaces every email over 10 MB — swap the number to size:25m or size:5m to widen or narrow the net.
  2. Select the checkbox next to each large email you don't need and click the Trash icon.
  3. Next, clear out Promotions and old newsletters — see how to delete all promotional emails in Gmail for the fastest way to clear that entire tab.
  4. Open Trash from the left sidebar and click Empty Trash Now. This is the step people skip — without it, deleted mail still counts against your quota for 30 days.

This gets most accounts back under the limit within a few minutes. But if the real issue is years of accumulated mail spread across dozens of senders — not just a few oversized attachments — a size search only catches part of it. For the fuller picture on where recurring bloat comes from, see how to free up Gmail storage fast.

What happens if I don't fix Gmail storage when it's full?

Nothing catastrophic happens to what's already there, but new mail stops arriving — senders get a bounce-back message instead of a delivered email, and you can't compose or send anything from that account either. If the account is also tied to Drive or Photos, uploads and backups from those services pause too, since they share the same 15 GB pool. The account doesn't get deleted or locked; it just stops accepting anything new until you're back under quota.

Should I buy more storage instead of cleaning up?

A Google One subscription is the fastest fix if you need to unblock email sending in the next five minutes and don't have time to clean up. But it's a ceiling raise, not a fix — if the account has been quietly accumulating years of newsletters, receipts, and notification mail, it'll hit the new ceiling eventually too. A quick cleanup first, even a partial one focused on the largest senders, often buys enough headroom that paying for storage becomes optional rather than urgent.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Gmail say my storage is full when my inbox doesn't look that big?

Gmail's 15 GB is shared across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos on the same account. Large video files in Drive or full-resolution photo backups often eat more space than years of email — check one.google.com/storage to see the actual breakdown.

How much space do I need to free up to start sending email again?

Any amount under the 15 GB limit works — Gmail blocks sending the moment you go over, but even a few megabytes of headroom restores the ability to send. Emptying Trash after a bulk delete is the fastest way to get under the limit immediately.

Does deleting emails free up space right away or after 30 days?

Deleting moves emails to Trash, where they still count against your quota for 30 days unless you empty Trash manually. If you're actively over the limit, delete the emails and then open Trash and click Empty Trash Now to release the space instantly.

Will buying more Google storage fix the problem permanently?

It buys time, not a fix. A Google One plan raises the ceiling, but if the underlying habit is never deleting anything, you'll hit the new ceiling eventually too. Clearing out what you don't need first is usually cheaper and keeps the account leaner long-term.

Can I see which emails are taking up the most space in Gmail?

Yes. Search size:10m in the Gmail search bar to find emails larger than 10 MB (adjust the number as needed). Large attachments — PDFs, videos, high-res images — are almost always the biggest single offenders, far more than plain text messages.
