How to Delete Google Alerts Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: To delete Google Alerts emails in Gmail, search from:googlealerts-noreply@google.com, click the top checkbox, choose "Select all conversations that match this search," and click Trash. Then go to google.com/alerts and delete or slow down the alerts you no longer read, or the emails will start piling up again tomorrow.

Google Alerts is easy to set up and easy to forget. You add an alert for your name, your company, a competitor, or a topic you were researching, and it quietly sends an email every day for years. Five alerts on "as-it-happens" can mean thousands of emails, most of them never opened.

The good news: every alert comes from the same address, so clearing them out takes a couple of minutes. The part most people skip is turning off the source.

How do I delete Google Alerts emails in Gmail?

  1. In the Gmail search bar, type from:googlealerts-noreply@google.com and press Enter. Add older_than:30d if you want to keep the last month of alerts.
  2. Scroll the results and star any alert that links to an article you still need. Then add -is:starred to the search and run it again so those drop out.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top of the list. Gmail selects only the 50 emails on the page, so click "Select all conversations that match this search" in the banner that appears.
  4. Click the Trash icon. The alerts move to Trash and stay recoverable for 30 days.

Want to clear one alert topic but keep the others? Add the topic in quotes, like from:googlealerts-noreply@google.com "your company name". Google puts the alert's search term in the subject line, so this narrows the results cleanly. For more ways to combine operators, see Gmail Search Operators to Find Old Emails.

How do I stop Google Alerts from emailing me?

Deleting the emails does nothing to the alerts themselves. They keep running until you change them. You have two options.

Delete the alert. Go to google.com/alerts while signed in to the same Google account. Under My alerts, you'll see every alert you've created. Click the trash can next to any alert you no longer need. It stops immediately.

Use the email link. Every alert email has an unsubscribe link at the bottom. Click it and that one alert is removed. This is the easiest route if you've got alerts tied to an old account or you can't find them on the Alerts page.

If you manage alerts across several Google accounts, check each one. Alerts belong to the account that created them, not the inbox they're delivered to.

Can I get fewer Google Alerts emails instead of none?

Yes. Most people don't want to lose the alert, they just want less of it. On google.com/alerts, click the pencil icon next to an alert, then Show options. These settings make the biggest difference:

  • How often: switch from "As-it-happens" to "At most once a day" or "At most once a week." One weekly digest replaces dozens of single emails.
  • How many: change "All results" to "Only the best results." This cuts duplicate and low-quality hits.
  • Sources: limit to News or Blogs if you're getting irrelevant web pages.
  • Deliver to: choose "RSS feed" instead of your email. The alert keeps working, but it shows up in a feed reader rather than Gmail.

Click Update alert to save. Changes apply to the next delivery.

How do I keep Google Alerts out of my inbox automatically?

If you still want the alerts but don't want them in your main inbox, a Gmail filter handles it.

Search from:googlealerts-noreply@google.com, click the filter icon on the right of the search bar, and choose Create filter. Check Skip the Inbox (Archive it) and Apply the label, then create a label like "Alerts." Check "Also apply filter to matching conversations" to tidy the existing ones too. The alerts collect in their own label, where you can skim them on your schedule.

Prefer them gone after you've had a chance to look? Pick Delete it in the filter instead, and every new alert goes straight to Trash, where it sits for 30 days before Gmail removes it. The full setup is in Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

One catch with archiving: archived emails still count toward your 15 GB of Google storage. Alert emails are small, but years of daily alerts add up. Once or twice a year, re-run the older_than:30d search and clear the backlog.

Frequently asked questions

What email address do Google Alerts come from?

Google Alerts are sent from googlealerts-noreply@google.com. Searching that exact address in Gmail finds every alert email without catching other Google messages like security notices or Drive shares.

Does deleting Google Alerts emails cancel the alert?

No. Deleting the emails only clears Gmail. The alert keeps running until you delete it at google.com/alerts or click the unsubscribe link at the bottom of an alert email.

How do I get Google Alerts without them going to my inbox?

Either change the alert's Deliver to setting from your email to RSS feed, or create a Gmail filter for from:googlealerts-noreply@google.com that skips the inbox and applies a label.

Can I recover Google Alerts emails I deleted by mistake?

Yes, for 30 days. Open Trash in Gmail, search for googlealerts-noreply@google.com, select the emails you want, and click Move to Inbox. After 30 days Gmail removes them permanently.

Why am I getting Google Alerts I never set up?

You may have created them years ago, or signed up on a shared device. Check google.com/alerts while signed in to each of your Google accounts. If an alert isn't listed there, use the unsubscribe link in the email itself.
