How to Delete Emails Older Than a Year in Gmail

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete emails older than a year in Gmail, search older_than:1y, click the checkbox above the message list, then click "Select all conversations that match this search" and hit the Trash icon. Add exclusions like -is:starred -has:attachment to the search first so nothing you need gets caught. The deleted mail sits in Trash for 30 days before it's gone for good.

A year is a sensible cutoff. Almost nothing from twelve months ago still needs to be one search away, and on a typical account that stretch of mail is where the newsletters, receipts, and notification noise have quietly stacked up. The mechanics take about two minutes — the part worth slowing down for is deciding what the search should not match.

How do I delete emails older than a year in Gmail?

  1. Open Gmail and type older_than:1y into the search bar, then press Enter.
  2. Scan the first page of results. This is your sanity check — if you see anything you'd miss, go back and refine the search before selecting anything.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the message list. That selects the 50 conversations on the current page.
  4. A banner appears above the list: "Select all conversations that match this search." Click it. The selection now covers every matching message, not just the visible page.
  5. Click the Trash icon and confirm when Gmail asks. Everything moves to Trash with a 30-day window to restore it.

Gmail also accepts older_than:12m and older_than:365d — all three return identical results, so use whichever you'll remember.

What should I exclude before deleting a year of email?

A bare older_than:1y is broader than most people expect. It matches on date alone, which means it sweeps in Sent mail, archived threads, and anything sitting in All Mail — not just your inbox. Build the search up in pieces instead:

  1. older_than:1y in:inbox — limits the cleanup to the inbox and leaves Sent and archived mail untouched.
  2. older_than:1y -is:starred -is:important — skips anything you or Gmail flagged as worth keeping.
  3. older_than:1y -has:attachment — protects old contracts, tax documents, and photos people sent you.
  4. older_than:1y -from:bank.com -from:irs.gov — excludes specific senders. Repeat the -from: term for each one.

These stack, so a cautious full search looks like older_than:1y in:inbox -is:starred -is:important -has:attachment. Run it, check the result count Gmail shows, and only then select all.

If you want the opposite of caution — clearing out attachments specifically because they're what's eating your quota — handle that as its own pass. How to Find the Biggest Emails in Gmail covers finding the heavy messages so you can review them individually instead of trashing them blind.

Why does Gmail's select-all fail on a year of old email?

Gmail's page checkbox only ever selects 50 conversations. The "select all conversations that match this search" banner is what extends it past that — and on a year's worth of mail, that's often tens of thousands of messages routed through a single browser request. It frequently stalls, times out, or reports success while only part of the batch actually moved.

The usual workaround is to shrink each batch so the request completes. Instead of one enormous delete, run the search quarter by quarter using fixed dates: before:2025/03/31 after:2024/12/31, then the next window, and so on. Slower, but each pass reliably finishes.

The other approach is to stop thinking in dates and think in senders. Old mail is rarely spread evenly — it's concentrated in a handful of senders that mailed you weekly for years. Clearing the top ten senders usually removes more old email than a date search does, and you can see exactly what you're deleting before you commit. How do I delete emails before a specific date instead?

Use before: with a date in YYYY/MM/DD format — for example before:2025/09/22. The difference matters more than it looks: older_than:1y is a rolling window that moves forward every day, while before: is a fixed line in the sand.

That makes before: the better choice when you're working through a big cleanup over several sessions, since the result set doesn't drift between runs. Pair it with after: to carve out a specific window: before:2025/01/01 after:2023/12/31 gives you exactly the 2024 calendar year.

Can Gmail delete emails older than a year automatically?

Not with a standard filter. Filters run once, when a message arrives, and a brand-new email is never a year old — so a filter with older_than:1y as its condition will match nothing, forever. This trips up a lot of people.

There are two workable options. The first is a Google Apps Script on a daily time-driven trigger that searches older_than:1y and moves the results to Trash; it runs on Google's servers, so nothing has to stay open on your machine. The second is simply calendaring the manual search four times a year, which is enough to keep a normal inbox from rebuilding a decade-deep backlog.

Filters are still worth setting up — just point them at the incoming mail you already know you don't want, so there's less to clean up a year from now. Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically walks through the conditions and actions in detail.

Does this actually free up storage?

Eventually. Deleting moves mail to Trash, and messages in Trash keep counting against your 15 GB for the full 30 days before Gmail purges them. If you're deleting because you're near the limit, open Trash and click Empty Trash now once you've confirmed nothing important is in there.

Be aware that a year of text-only email may free up less space than you'd hope — a few thousand plain messages can amount to well under a gigabyte. Attachments are almost always where the storage actually went.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Gmail search for emails older than one year?

Type older_than:1y in the search bar. Gmail also accepts older_than:12m and older_than:365d, which return the same results. The operator counts back from today, so the cutoff moves forward every day you run it.

Does older_than:1y include emails I sent?

Yes. The operator matches on date, not location, so it covers Sent, Archive, All Mail, Spam, and Trash as well as your inbox. Add in:inbox to restrict it to the inbox only.

How do I delete emails from before a specific date instead?

Use before:2025/09/22 with the date in YYYY/MM/DD format. Unlike older_than:1y, this is a fixed cutoff that doesn't shift as time passes, which makes it better for a repeatable cleanup.

Will deleting a year of old email free up Gmail storage right away?

No. Deleted mail sits in Trash for 30 days and keeps counting against your 15 GB until then. Open Trash and click "Empty Trash now" if you need the space immediately.

Can I make Gmail delete emails older than a year automatically?

Not with a plain filter — filters only run on incoming mail, and a message isn't a year old when it arrives. Use a Google Apps Script on a daily trigger that searches older_than:1y and trashes the results, or repeat the manual search every few months.
