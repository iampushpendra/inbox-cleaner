How to Mass Delete Emails in Gmail

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To mass delete emails in Gmail, run a search that isolates the batch you want gone — such as category:promotions older_than:1y — click the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit Trash. Do it in several targeted passes rather than one giant delete, then empty Trash to reclaim the storage. Mass deleting in Gmail fails for one of two reasons. Either you try to delete from the open inbox view and Gmail hands you 50 emails at a time, or you throw one enormous search at it and the select-all banner quietly gives up halfway through. Both are avoidable. The trick is deciding what the batch is before you start clicking.

How do I mass delete emails in Gmail?

Always start from the search bar, not the inbox list. A search defines the batch precisely, and it's the only way to tell Gmail "everything matching this," not "everything on this screen."

  1. Type a query into the Gmail search bar that matches exactly what you want to remove — category:promotions older_than:1y, from:noreply@example.com, or larger:10M.
  2. Press Enter and read the conversation count at the top right of the list. If a search you expected to match 2,000 emails returns 40,000, stop and narrow it.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the message list. That selects the 50 conversations on screen.
  4. Click the banner that appears: "Select all conversations that match this search." This is the step most people miss, and it's the one that turns a page delete into a mass delete.
  5. Click the Trash icon and wait for Gmail to confirm before starting anything else.

Gmail processes large deletions server-side, so the page can look finished while work is still happening in the background. Give it a minute and refresh before you decide it didn't work.

What should I mass delete first?

Order matters, because the first two or three passes usually account for most of the clutter. Work down this list, running each as its own search and delete:

  • category:promotions older_than:6m — marketing mail you were never going to reread.
  • category:social older_than:6m — notification emails from social platforms.
  • from:notifications@example.com — one dead app or service at a time.
  • larger:10M — a few hundred of these can outweigh 50,000 text emails for storage.
  • older_than:3y -is:starred — a broad sweep of old mail, with anything you flagged excluded.

Add -is:starred to any broad query as a matter of habit. It costs nothing and it's the cheapest insurance against deleting something you'd actually miss. Why does Gmail only delete 50 emails at a time?

Gmail renders 50 conversations per page, and the header checkbox can only act on what's rendered. The "select all conversations that match this search" banner is Gmail's workaround — it hands the delete to the server instead of the browser. But that path has its own ceiling: on searches matching tens of thousands of messages, it frequently times out or completes only part of the batch, leaving you to run the same delete three or four times. The full mechanics of the cap are broken down in How to Select All Emails in Gmail.

The manual fix is to slice the batch by date. Instead of older_than:3y in one go, run after:2018/01/01 before:2019/01/01, delete, then step forward a year at a time. Each batch is small enough to finish reliably. It works — it just turns one action into ten.

Do I need to empty Trash after a mass delete?

Yes, if storage was the point. Deleted mail moves to Trash and keeps counting against your 15 GB until Gmail purges it automatically after 30 days. To reclaim space now, open Trash in the left sidebar and click Empty Trash now.

That 30-day window is your safety net, so use it deliberately. Do all your deleting passes first, spend a few minutes checking that nothing important vanished, and empty Trash only once you're satisfied. If something did go missing, search in:trash with the query you deleted with, select the results, and move them back to the inbox — the recovery is as fast as the delete was.

Frequently asked questions

How many emails can Gmail mass delete at one time?

Gmail doesn't publish a hard number. The page checkbox selects 50, and the "select all conversations" banner extends that to the whole search result — but batches in the tens of thousands often stall or finish only partway. Splitting a huge search into smaller date ranges is more reliable.

Why is my Gmail mass delete taking so long to finish?

Gmail processes the deletion server-side in the background, so a batch of tens of thousands can keep running after the page looks idle. Leave the tab open, avoid starting a second delete on top of it, and refresh after a few minutes to see the updated count.

Does mass deleting emails free up Gmail storage immediately?

No. Deleted mail sits in Trash and still counts against your 15 GB until it's purged. Open Trash and click "Empty Trash now" to reclaim the space right away, and note that storage figures can take a few hours to update.

Can I mass delete emails in Gmail from my phone?

Not really. The Gmail mobile app has no "select all conversations that match this search" option, so you're limited to tapping messages one by one. Use a desktop browser for mass deletes, or open the tool in your phone's browser instead of the app.

Can I undo a mass delete in Gmail?

Yes, for 30 days. Everything you deleted is in Trash — search in:trash with the same query you used, select the results, and click "Move to" to put them back in your inbox before the window closes.
