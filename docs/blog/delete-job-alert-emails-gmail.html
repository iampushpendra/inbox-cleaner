How to Delete Job Alert Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete job alert emails in Gmail, search from:(jobalerts-noreply@linkedin.com OR indeed.com OR glassdoor.com OR ziprecruiter.com OR monster.com) -subject:(interview OR application OR offer), click the top checkbox, choose "Select all conversations that match this search," and click Trash. The -subject part keeps real replies about your applications. Then turn off the alerts on each job board so they stop coming back.

Job hunting is stressful enough without your inbox turning into a second job board. You search for "product manager" once, and suddenly LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, and ZipRecruiter all email you every morning. Some send two or three alerts a day. A few months later you've got thousands of them.

The worst part is that real emails get buried. A recruiter's reply or an interview invite can sit under 40 "New jobs for you" messages. Clearing out the alerts makes the emails that matter easy to find again.

How do I delete job alert emails in Gmail?

Search by sender. Job alerts come from a small number of addresses, so one search catches most of them.

  1. In the Gmail search bar, paste from:(jobalerts-noreply@linkedin.com OR indeed.com OR glassdoor.com OR ziprecruiter.com OR monster.com) and press Enter.
  2. Add -subject:(interview OR application OR offer) to the end. This keeps application confirmations and recruiter messages out of the results. Star anything else you want to keep, then add -is:starred.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top of the message list. Gmail selects the 50 emails on screen.
  4. Click the banner that says "Select all conversations that match this search" to extend the selection to every match.
  5. Click the Trash icon. Everything moves to Trash, where it stays recoverable for 30 days.

Then run a second pass by subject line: subject:("job alert" OR "jobs for you" OR "new jobs" OR "recommended jobs"). This catches smaller boards, niche sites like Dice or Wellfound, and company career pages that send their own alerts.

Note that the LinkedIn address above only matches job alerts. LinkedIn also sends connection requests, "who viewed your profile," and post notifications from other addresses. To clear those too, see How to Delete LinkedIn Notification Emails in Gmail.

Which job emails should I keep?

Job alerts are almost pure noise once a few days have passed. Listings close fast, and every board keeps the live version on its site. But job hunting creates other emails that look similar and do matter:

  • Application confirmations. "Your application was sent to Acme" is proof you applied. Keep these until the process ends.
  • Recruiter messages. InMail notifications and direct recruiter emails often include contact details you won't find elsewhere.
  • Interview invites and offers. Calendar links, offer letters, and start dates belong in a label, not the Trash.
  • Assessment links. Coding tests and personality assessments often expire, and the link only lives in the email.

A simple rule: keep anything addressed to you as a candidate, delete anything that's a list of jobs. If you're still searching, you might also add older_than:14d to the search so this week's alerts stay put.

How do I stop job alert emails from coming back?

Deleting clears the backlog. Stopping them takes a few minutes on each site, because every saved search is a separate alert.

LinkedIn. Open Jobs, then Job alerts, and delete each saved alert. To shut them off completely, go to Settings, Communications, Email, and turn off job alert emails.

Indeed. Sign in, open your profile menu, and choose Job alerts or Communication settings. Delete each alert, or switch frequency from daily to weekly.

Glassdoor and ZipRecruiter. Both have an "Email settings" or "Job alerts" page under your account. The unsubscribe link at the bottom of any alert email also takes you there.

If you'd rather not log in to each site, Gmail's own Unsubscribe link next to the sender name works for most boards. How to Unsubscribe from Emails in Gmail walks through that and what to do when the link doesn't appear.

Can Gmail filter job alerts automatically?

Yes. If you're still job hunting and want alerts out of the inbox but still searchable, a filter is the right tool. Paste the sender search from above into the search bar, click the filter icon, and choose Create filter. Pick Skip the Inbox and Apply the label "Job alerts." New alerts land in that label, and you check it when you have time.

Once you've landed a role, edit the filter to Delete it instead, or remove it after turning the alerts off. For more filter recipes, see Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Will deleting job alert emails delete my job applications?

No. Your applications live in your account on LinkedIn, Indeed, or the employer's career site. Deleting the alert email only removes the copy in Gmail. Just keep confirmation and recruiter emails if you rely on them as your record.

Why do I keep getting job alerts I never signed up for?

Most job boards turn on alerts automatically when you search for a role, upload a resume, or apply through them. Each saved search becomes its own alert, so one afternoon of job hunting can create several daily emails.

How do I turn off LinkedIn job alerts?

On LinkedIn, go to Jobs, then Job alerts (or Preferences), and delete each saved alert. You can also go to Settings, then Communications, then Email, and turn off job alert emails entirely.

Can I pause job alerts instead of deleting them?

Yes. Indeed and LinkedIn both let you change alert frequency from daily to weekly, which cuts volume by about 85 percent. A Gmail filter that skips the inbox is another way to keep them searchable without seeing them.

Should I delete job alerts after I get a new job?

Yes, and switch the alerts off at the source at the same time. Old alerts have no value once the listings expire, and most job postings close within 30 days.
