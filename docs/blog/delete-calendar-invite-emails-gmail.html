How to Delete Calendar Invite Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: To delete calendar invite emails in Gmail, search filename:ics older_than:7d, click the top checkbox, choose "Select all conversations that match this search," and click Trash. The events stay on your Google Calendar, so you lose nothing but the emails. Then turn off guest-response and change notifications in Calendar settings to stop new ones piling up.

Every meeting generates mail. There's the invitation, then an "Updated invitation" when someone moves it by 15 minutes, then a "Canceled event," then a stack of "Accepted" and "Declined" replies if you're the organizer. Run a few recurring meetings for a couple of years and calendar email can quietly become one of the biggest categories in your inbox.

None of it needs to stay. Once an event is on your calendar, the email that delivered it is just a copy.

How do I delete calendar invite emails in Gmail?

Calendar invites almost always carry an .ics file, whether they come from Google Calendar, Outlook, Zoom, or a booking tool. That gives you one search that catches nearly all of them.

  1. In the Gmail search bar, type filename:ics older_than:7d and press Enter. The older_than:7d part keeps this week's invites in your inbox in case you haven't responded yet.
  2. Scroll through the results and star anything you still need, like an invite with a long agenda or a dial-in you reuse. Then add -is:starred to the search and run it again.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top of the list. Gmail only selects the 50 emails on the page, so click "Select all conversations that match this search" in the banner that appears.
  4. Click the Trash icon. The invite emails move to Trash and stay recoverable for 30 days.

Some tools send invites without an attachment and put the details in the subject instead. To sweep those up, run a second search: subject:("Invitation:" OR "Updated invitation" OR "Canceled event" OR "Accepted:" OR "Declined:") older_than:7d. For more ways to stack operators like these, see Gmail Search Operators to Find Old Emails.

Will deleting the invite email remove the event from my calendar?

No. This is the question that stops most people, and the answer is reassuring. When an invite arrives, Google Calendar adds the event to your calendar on its own. From then on, the event and the email are separate. Trashing the email doesn't delete the event, doesn't change your RSVP, and doesn't notify the organizer.

You can still respond any time by opening the event in Calendar and picking Yes, No, or Maybe. Updates from the organizer keep syncing to the event whether the original email exists or not.

One exception: if you've set Calendar to add invitations only after you respond in email, an invite you never answered won't be on your calendar. That's why the steps above skip the last seven days. Anything older than a week is almost certainly in the past or already handled.

How do I stop calendar notification emails from piling up?

Clearing the backlog is half the job. The other half is cutting how many arrive. Open Google Calendar, click the gear icon, and choose Settings. Under Settings for my calendars, click your calendar, then scroll to Other notifications. Set these to None:

  • Guest responses to events: the "Accepted" and "Declined" emails you get as an organizer. You can see the responses inside the event.
  • Changed events: emails when someone else edits an event you're invited to.
  • Canceled events: the event disappears from your calendar anyway.
  • Daily agenda: a morning summary email most people never open.

Leave New events on if you want to know when someone invites you. That's the one email worth getting.

How do I keep calendar invites out of my inbox automatically?

A Gmail filter can tidy invites as they arrive. Search from:calendar-notification@google.com, click the filter icon on the right of the search bar, and choose Create filter. Check Skip the Inbox (Archive it) and Apply the label with a label like "Calendar." Check "Also apply filter to matching conversations" to clean up the existing ones too.

Don't filter filename:ics into Trash automatically. You'd miss new invites if your calendar only adds events after you respond. Label them instead, then clear the label every few months. The full setup is in Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

Archived emails still count toward your 15 GB of Google storage. Invite emails are small, but each one carries an attachment, and years of recurring meetings add up. Frequently asked questions

If I delete a calendar invite email, does the event disappear from my calendar?

No. Once Google Calendar has added the event, it lives in Calendar independently of the email. Deleting the invite email removes only the message, not the event or your RSVP.

Can I still RSVP after deleting the invite email?

Yes. Open the event in Google Calendar and choose Yes, No, or Maybe there. The RSVP buttons in the email are just a shortcut to the same event.

How do I stop getting Accepted and Declined emails for meetings I organize?

In Google Calendar, open Settings, pick your calendar under Settings for my calendars, and go to Other notifications. Set Guest responses to None. You can still see who accepted inside the event.

Will deleting the email decline the meeting for me?

No. Deleting an invite email sends nothing to the organizer. Your response stays whatever it was, including no response at all. To decline, open the event in Calendar and click No.

How do I find calendar invites from Outlook or Zoom users in Gmail?

They also carry an .ics file, so filename:ics finds them. If a few slip through, search subject:("Invitation:" OR "Updated invitation" OR "Canceled") to catch them by subject line.
