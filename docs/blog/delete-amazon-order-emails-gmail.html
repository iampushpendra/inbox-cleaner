How to Delete Amazon Order Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete Amazon order emails in Gmail, search from:amazon.com, click the top checkbox, choose "Select all conversations that match this search," and click Trash. Add older_than:1y to keep recent orders for returns. If you have thousands of Amazon emails and Gmail's select-all times out, Inbox Cleaner clears every Amazon sender in one click.

Every Amazon order sends you at least three emails: an order confirmation, a "shipped" notice, and a "delivered" notice. Add returns, refunds, Subscribe & Save reminders, review requests, and deal alerts, and a regular shopper can rack up a few thousand Amazon emails in a couple of years. Almost none of them are worth keeping once the package has arrived.

The good news: Amazon emails are easy to target, because they all come from one domain. The catch is that Amazon uses a dozen different sending addresses, and Gmail's bulk tools still struggle once the numbers get large.

How do I delete all Amazon order emails in Gmail?

  1. In the Gmail search bar, type from:amazon.com and press Enter. This matches every Amazon address at once — auto-confirm@amazon.com, shipment-tracking@amazon.com, order-update@amazon.com, store-news@amazon.com, and the rest.
  2. Optional: narrow the search. Add older_than:1y to keep the last year of orders, or add subject:(shipped OR delivered) to target only shipping notices.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top of the message list. Gmail selects the 50 emails on screen.
  4. Click the banner that says "Select all conversations that match this search" to extend the selection to every match.
  5. Click the Trash icon. Everything selected moves to Trash, where it stays recoverable for 30 days.

If you're outside the US, swap in your local Amazon domain: from:amazon.co.uk, from:amazon.de, from:amazon.in, and so on. To catch several at once, use from:(amazon.com OR amazon.co.uk). For more ways to combine operators like these, see Gmail Search Operators to Find Old Emails.

Which Amazon emails should I keep before deleting?

Most Amazon emails are safe to delete because Amazon keeps the real record. Your full order history, invoices, and tracking details live in your Amazon account under Returns & Orders, and you can download an invoice for most past orders whenever you need one.

A few are worth a second look:

  • Recent orders still inside the return window. The older_than:1y filter (or older_than:3m) keeps these out of the delete.
  • Big purchases with warranties. Electronics and appliances sometimes need proof of purchase. Star these first, then add -is:starred to your search so they're skipped.
  • Business or tax expenses. If you claim Amazon purchases on taxes, download invoices from your Amazon account before clearing the emails.
  • Gift card and digital code emails. Unredeemed codes sometimes only exist in the email. Search from:amazon.com gift card and check before you delete.

A safe all-in-one search looks like this: from:amazon.com older_than:6m -is:starred. It catches the old clutter and leaves anything recent or flagged.

How do I stop Amazon order emails from filling up Gmail again?

Clearing the backlog is half the job. New orders will start the pile again, so set up two things.

Trim Amazon's own email settings. In your Amazon account, open Communication Preferences and turn off promotional categories — deals, recommendations, and "customers who bought" emails. Amazon still sends order confirmations and shipping updates, but this cuts the marketing noise.

Create a Gmail filter for shipping notices. Search from:shipment-tracking@amazon.com, click the filter icon in the search bar, then choose Create filter. Pick Skip the Inbox to archive them automatically, or Delete it if you never look at them. Delivery notices are only useful for a day or two, so they're the easiest category to automate. For more filter recipes, read Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

One caution: Gmail filters can't use older_than on incoming mail in a useful way, since new messages are never older than a day when they arrive. If you want a rolling cleanup of old Amazon emails, re-run your from:amazon.com older_than:6m search every few months.

Should I archive Amazon emails instead of deleting them?

Archiving removes emails from your inbox but keeps them in All Mail, so they still count against your 15 GB of Google storage. Amazon emails are small, but thousands of image-heavy order and deal emails add up. If storage matters, delete. If you like being able to search old orders from Gmail, archive. Frequently asked questions

Will deleting Amazon emails in Gmail delete my Amazon order history?

No. Your order history lives in your Amazon account under Returns & Orders, not in Gmail. Deleting the emails only removes Gmail's copy of the notification.

Can I get an Amazon receipt again after deleting the email?

Yes. Open Returns & Orders on Amazon, find the order, and choose View invoice or Print invoice. You can download a fresh receipt for most past orders at any time.

How do I stop Amazon from sending so many shipping emails?

Go to Amazon's Communication Preferences page in your account settings and turn off the categories you don't want, such as promotions and recommendations. Order confirmations are usually still sent, so pair this with a Gmail filter for the rest.

Does from:amazon.com catch Amazon Prime Video and Kindle emails too?

It catches anything sent from an amazon.com address, which includes most Kindle and Prime emails. Some services use other domains, such as primevideo.com or audible.com, so search those separately if they matter to you.

Are fake Amazon emails caught by this search?

Mostly not. Phishing emails that pretend to be Amazon usually come from look-alike domains, so from:amazon.com won't match them. Report those as phishing in Gmail instead of just deleting them.
