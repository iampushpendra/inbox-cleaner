How to Delete Gmail Emails by Size

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete Gmail emails by size, search larger:10M, tick the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search", and hit Trash. Then step down through larger:5M and larger:2M to catch the next tier. None of it frees storage until you open Trash and click Empty Trash now.

Gmail gives you 15 GB and no way to sort by size, which is a frustrating combination when you're staring at a quota warning. The fix is to stop looking for a size column and start deleting in bands: run one search per size threshold, clear it, then loosen the threshold and go again. Done in that order, most accounts drop a few gigabytes in about ten minutes.

How do I delete Gmail emails by size?

Work top-down. The first pass returns a handful of obviously oversized emails you can judge at a glance, and each pass after that gets larger in count and smaller in payoff.

  1. Click the Gmail search bar, type larger:10M, and press Enter. This searches your whole account — Sent and archived mail included, not just the inbox.
  2. Skim the results and download anything you still want. Gmail can't strip an attachment and keep the message, so deleting the email deletes the file with it.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the list. That selects the 50 emails on the current page only.
  4. Click the banner that appears — "Select all conversations that match this search" — to extend the selection to every matching email.
  5. Click the Trash icon and confirm the bulk action. Gmail processes large selections in the background, so the count may keep ticking down for a minute after you click.
  6. Repeat with larger:5M, then larger:2M, then larger:1M, reviewing each batch before you delete it.
  7. Open Trash in the left sidebar and click Empty Trash now. Do the same in Spam. Until both are empty, the space is still counted against you.

That last step is the one people skip. Deleting an email in Gmail moves it to Trash, where it keeps occupying quota for 30 days. If you delete 4 GB of attachments and your storage meter doesn't budge, this is almost always why.

Which size threshold should I start with?

Start at larger:10M and only go lower if the result count is small. Ten emails at 15 MB each is 150 MB — real, but not a rescue. The band that usually moves the needle is larger:1M older_than:2y: old attachments you've already downloaded, already acted on, and will never open again.

A few operators make the batches easier to judge:

  1. larger:1M smaller:5M brackets a range, so you can clear the mid-tier without re-reviewing the big emails you already handled.
  2. larger:5M in:sent shows files you sent. These are the easiest deletions in your account — you already have the originals on your own machine.
  3. larger:2M has:attachment filters out long text threads that crossed the threshold from message history rather than files.
  4. larger:2M filename:mp4 (or zip, pptx, psd) targets the heavy file types directly, which is faster than eyeballing a mixed list.

Why is my storage still full after deleting large emails?

Three usual causes, in order of likelihood.

Trash and Spam are still holding it. Both count toward your 15 GB. Empty both manually rather than waiting out the 30-day auto-purge.

The meter lags. Google's storage page doesn't update instantly after a large deletion. Give it a few minutes, and occasionally a few hours, before assuming nothing happened.

Gmail wasn't the problem. That 15 GB is shared across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos. Check the breakdown at one.google.com/storage — if Photos is using 11 GB, no amount of email deleting will fix it. How to Free Up Gmail Storage Fast covers the full triage order across all three services.

How do I delete large emails from the Gmail app on my phone?

You can search by size on mobile — type larger:10M into the app's search bar exactly as you would on desktop, and the operator works. What you can't do is select everything at once. The mobile app has no "select all conversations that match this search" banner, so you're long-pressing messages one by one.

For anything past a couple of dozen emails, open Gmail in a desktop browser instead. If you only have a phone, the mobile browser in desktop mode gets you the select-all banner, though it's fiddly on a small screen. Emptying Trash works fine on mobile: open the menu, tap Trash, then Empty trash now.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between larger: and size: in Gmail search?

Nothing practical — larger:10M and size:10M return the same results. Both accept M for megabytes, K for kilobytes, and a bare number for bytes. There's also smaller:, which you can combine with larger: to bracket a size range.

Can I sort Gmail by attachment size?

No. Gmail sorts by date only and has never offered a size column. Searching in size bands with larger: is the closest equivalent, and bracketing with larger:20M smaller:25M narrows a specific message down to a range.

How much storage does a normal email use?

A plain text email is usually a few kilobytes, so tens of thousands of them add up to well under a gigabyte. Attachments are what consume real space — a single 20 MB video is worth roughly 4,000 ordinary emails.

Do emails in Spam and Trash count toward my Gmail storage?

Yes. Both folders count against your 15 GB until they're emptied. Gmail clears each automatically after 30 days, but if you want the space back now, empty both manually.

Does deleting large Gmail emails free up Google Drive space too?

It frees space in the shared 15 GB pool that Gmail, Drive, and Google Photos all draw from, so Drive gets more room to work with. It doesn't delete anything stored in Drive itself.
