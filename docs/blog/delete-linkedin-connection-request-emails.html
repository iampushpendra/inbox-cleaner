How to Stop and Delete LinkedIn Connection Request Emails

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: To delete LinkedIn connection request emails, search Gmail for from:invitations@linkedin.com -is:starred, tick the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search," and click Trash. To stop them, go to LinkedIn's Settings & Privacy, open Notifications, choose Email, and switch off invitation emails. Requests still show up on LinkedIn; only the Gmail copies stop.

Connection requests are some of LinkedIn's noisiest emails. Every invite arrives as its own message. Then, if you don't respond, LinkedIn sends a reminder. Sometimes it sends two. Recruiters, salespeople, and strangers you've never met all add to the pile.

The emails add nothing. You can't accept a request from Gmail without opening LinkedIn anyway, and the request lives on LinkedIn whether the email exists or not. So the email is safe to delete, and safe to switch off.

How do I stop and delete LinkedIn connection request emails?

LinkedIn sends invitations and invitation reminders from one address: invitations@linkedin.com. That makes the cleanup a single search.

  1. In the Gmail search bar, type from:invitations@linkedin.com -is:starred and press Enter. The -is:starred part protects any invite you flagged to follow up on.
  2. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the message list. Gmail selects the 50 emails on screen.
  3. Click the banner that says "Select all conversations that match this search." Now the selection covers every invitation email you have.
  4. Click the Trash icon. The emails move to Trash and stay recoverable for 30 days.
  5. On LinkedIn, open Settings & Privacy, go to Notifications, choose Email, and switch off invitation emails. This is the step that keeps them from coming back.

Want to clear out profile views, job alerts, and post notifications too? Search from:linkedin.com instead. How to Delete LinkedIn Notification Emails in Gmail breaks down every LinkedIn sending address and which ones are worth keeping.

Why does LinkedIn keep sending me connection request reminders?

LinkedIn wants you to answer every invitation. If you leave one pending, it follows up with reminder emails like "Sarah is waiting for your response." These come from the same invitations@linkedin.com address, so the search above catches them.

Deleting the email doesn't answer the request, though. The fastest way to stop reminders for a specific person is to deal with the invite on LinkedIn:

  • Accept it if you know them. The reminders stop.
  • Ignore it if you don't. LinkedIn doesn't tell the sender, and the reminders stop.
  • Report or block the sender if the request is spam or a fake profile.

Go to My Network, open Invitations, and clear the whole list in a few minutes. Once nothing is pending, LinkedIn has nothing to remind you about.

How do I turn off LinkedIn invitation emails?

LinkedIn's email settings are split into categories. Invitations have their own toggle, so you can turn them off without losing message alerts or anything else you want to keep.

Click your profile photo, then Settings & Privacy. Open Notifications and pick Email. Look for the section covering invitations and messages, then switch off emails for connection invitations. LinkedIn moves these menus around every so often. If you can't find it, type "email" into the settings search box.

You'll still see every request in the LinkedIn app and under My Network. Only the email copy stops.

Can I stop strangers from sending me connection requests at all?

Partly. LinkedIn lets you limit who can send you invitations. In Settings & Privacy, look for the section about who can reach you, then open the invitations option. You can restrict requests to people who know your email address or who appear in your imported contacts.

This cuts down on cold requests from recruiters and sales reps. The trade-off is that real contacts who don't have your email will find it harder to connect. If you're job hunting, you may want to leave it open for now.

Can Gmail delete LinkedIn invitation emails automatically?

Yes. If you'd rather keep invitation emails on but not let them pile up, set a Gmail filter. Type from:invitations@linkedin.com older_than:14d into the search bar, open the search options, and click Create filter. Tick Delete it, then Also apply filter to matching conversations.

New invites still reach your inbox, so you won't miss one from someone you know. After two weeks they clear themselves out. For more recipes and how to avoid deleting the wrong thing, see Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

LinkedIn also sends marketing emails, like Premium offers, that have nothing to do with your settings. Use the unsubscribe link at the bottom of those. How to Unsubscribe from Emails in Gmail covers what to do when the link is hidden or missing.

Frequently asked questions

Does deleting a LinkedIn invitation email decline the connection request?

No. The request stays pending on LinkedIn until you accept or ignore it there. Deleting the email only removes the copy in Gmail.

Why does LinkedIn keep emailing me about the same connection request?

LinkedIn sends reminder emails for invitations you haven't answered. Accept or ignore the request on LinkedIn and the reminders for that person stop.

Will the sender know if I ignore their LinkedIn connection request?

No. LinkedIn doesn't notify people when you ignore their invitation. Their request simply stays unanswered from their side.

Can I stop strangers from sending me LinkedIn connection requests?

You can limit them. In LinkedIn's Settings & Privacy, look for the invitations option under who can reach you, and restrict requests to people who know your email address or appear in your imported contacts.

Will turning off invitation emails hide connection requests on LinkedIn?

No. Requests still appear under My Network on LinkedIn and in the app. Turning off the email only stops the Gmail copy.
