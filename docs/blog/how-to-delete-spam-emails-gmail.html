How to Delete Spam Emails in Gmail

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete spam emails in Gmail, open the Spam folder in the left sidebar and click "Delete all spam messages now" at the top of the list. That empties the whole folder in one action — no 50-per-page limit — and the messages are deleted permanently rather than moved to Trash. Spam that landed in your inbox instead needs the Report spam button, which both removes it and trains Gmail's filter.

Spam in Gmail is actually two separate problems. There's the Spam folder, which Gmail fills automatically and clears on a 30-day timer. And there's the junk that gets past the filter and sits in your inbox, which Gmail won't touch until you tell it to. They're cleaned up differently, so it's worth handling both.

How do I delete all spam emails in Gmail?

  1. Open Gmail on desktop. In the left sidebar, click More to expand the full folder list.
  2. Click Spam.
  3. At the top of the message list, click "Delete all spam messages now."
  4. Click OK in the confirmation dialog. The folder empties instantly.

This is one of the few places where Gmail gives you a genuine one-click bulk action. You don't need the checkbox, you don't need the "select all conversations" banner, and it doesn't cap out at a page of 50 the way selecting all emails in Gmail does everywhere else in the app.

One important difference from the rest of Gmail: emptying Spam deletes permanently. Those messages don't land in Trash, and there's no 30-day recovery window. Take ten seconds to scan the sender column first — Gmail's filter is good but not perfect, and password resets or invoices from an unfamiliar domain occasionally end up misfiled.

Does the Gmail spam folder delete itself automatically?

Yes. Gmail permanently removes anything that's been in the Spam folder for more than 30 days, without any action from you. The folder never grows without bound.

So why empty it manually? Two reasons. First, spam counts against your 15 GB storage quota for the entire 30 days it sits there, which matters if you're close to the limit. Second, a spam folder showing 2,000 unread messages makes it impossible to spot the one legitimate email that got misfiled. Clearing it regularly turns it into a folder you can actually skim.

How do I delete spam that landed in my inbox?

Filter misses are the harder problem. These messages are in your inbox, they don't respond to "Delete all spam messages now," and Gmail won't remove them on a timer.

For a handful, select the checkboxes and click the Report spam button (the octagon with an exclamation mark) in the toolbar. That moves them to Spam and tells Gmail's classifier those messages were unwanted, so the next batch from that campaign is more likely to get caught.

How do I stop spam emails from coming back?

Deleting spam is maintenance, not a fix. Three things actually reduce the inflow:

Report, don't just delete. Deleting a spam message removes it and teaches Gmail nothing. Reporting it feeds the filter. Over a few weeks this makes a visible difference in how much gets through.

Block the sender when it's a real company. Open the message, click the three-dot menu, and choose Block [sender]. Everything from that address goes straight to Spam from then on. This works well for a legitimate business you've stopped caring about and poorly for actual spammers, who rotate addresses constantly.

Set up a filter for the patterns that repeat. If the same phrase or domain keeps showing up, build a filter that deletes it on arrival — search for the pattern, click the dropdown arrow in the search bar, choose Create filter, and check Delete it. See Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically for the full set of conditions and actions.

One caution on blocking: don't confuse spam with marketing you signed up for. Retailer emails and newsletters aren't spam, and reporting them as spam hurts the sender's reputation over something you consented to. Unsubscribe instead, or clear them in bulk as described in How to Delete All Promotional Emails in Gmail.

Does deleting spam free up Gmail storage?

Yes, but usually less than people expect. Spam messages are typically small — text and images pulled from remote servers rather than heavy attachments — so 3,000 spam emails might only account for a few hundred megabytes. It's real space, and it's free to reclaim, but it won't rescue an account sitting at 14.9 GB on its own.

If storage is the actual reason you're here, empty Spam first because it takes five seconds, then go after attachments, which is where the volume really is. Frequently asked questions

Can I recover an email I deleted from the Gmail spam folder?

No. Emptying the Spam folder deletes permanently — those messages skip Trash entirely. Scan the sender column before you click "Delete all spam messages now" if you're expecting something that may have been misfiled.

Why do I still get spam after blocking the sender?

Spammers rotate sending addresses and domains, so blocking one address rarely stops the campaign. Reporting as spam trains Gmail's filter on the message's fingerprint, which works better over time than blocking addresses one by one.

Does spam count toward my 15 GB Gmail storage limit?

Yes. Messages in Spam and Trash count against your quota until they're permanently removed. Gmail clears both folders automatically after 30 days, but emptying Spam manually frees that space right away.

Is it safe to delete everything in the Gmail spam folder?

Generally yes, but Gmail's filter occasionally misfiles legitimate mail — password resets, invoices, and messages from new contacts are the usual false positives. Skim the sender names for 10 seconds before emptying the folder.

What's the difference between deleting spam and reporting spam?

Deleting only removes the message. Reporting spam removes it and teaches Gmail's filter, so similar messages get routed to the Spam folder automatically instead of reaching your inbox.
