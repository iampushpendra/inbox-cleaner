Gmail Mailbox Full and Can't Receive Emails? What to Do

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: When your Gmail mailbox is full and you can't receive emails, you need to get back under your 15 GB Google storage limit. Empty Trash and Spam first, delete large attachments with the search larger:10M, then empty Trash again. New mail starts arriving as soon as you're under the limit, but anything that bounced while you were full is gone and has to be resent.

A full Gmail account doesn't just nag you with a banner. It stops working. Incoming messages bounce back to the sender, you can't send anything, and you may not notice until someone texts you asking why their email failed. That makes this an emergency, not a spring-cleaning job. The goal is to free enough space right now to get mail flowing, then deal with the backlog properly.

What should I do when my Gmail mailbox is full and I can't receive emails?

Work through these in order. The first two steps take under a minute and often free enough space on their own.

  1. Empty Trash and Spam. Open the Trash folder and click Empty Trash now, then do the same in Spam. Both count against your storage, and most people have gigabytes sitting there.
  2. Check what's actually full. Go to one.google.com/storage. Your 15 GB is shared across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos. If Photos is using 12 GB, deleting emails alone won't fix it.
  3. Delete large attachments. Search Gmail for larger:10M, select everything, and delete. Try larger:5M next if you need more room.
  4. Clear high-volume senders. Newsletters, promotions, and notification emails add up over years. Delete them in bulk by sender or by category.
  5. Empty Trash again. Everything you just deleted went to Trash, which still counts toward your quota. Empty it so the space is released.
  6. Send yourself a test email. Use another account or ask a friend. If it arrives, you're back in business.

Step five is the one people miss. Deleting in Gmail is a two-stage process, and until Trash is emptied your storage number barely moves. How to Permanently Delete Gmail Emails covers exactly how that works and when it's safe to skip the 30-day window.

Why does Gmail stop receiving emails when storage is full?

Gmail has nowhere to put new messages. Once your account passes its storage limit, Google rejects incoming mail at the server instead of queuing it. The sender gets an automatic bounce that says the account they tried to reach is over quota.

That has two practical consequences. First, bounced emails are not waiting for you somewhere. They were never delivered, so freeing space won't bring them back. Second, the people emailing you may assume your address is dead. If you're expecting anything important, like a job offer, a verification code, or a client reply, tell those senders to resend once you've fixed it.

How do I free up Gmail space fast enough to get email working today?

Go for size before volume. A single 25 MB attachment weighs as much as thousands of plain-text newsletters, so large-file searches give you the biggest win per click. Beyond larger:, the has:attachment older_than:2y search finds old attachments you almost certainly don't need.

If size searches don't get you far enough, move to volume. Gmail's select-all checkbox only grabs 50 emails per page. The "Select all conversations that match this search" banner extends it to every match, but on big batches it often stalls or times out partway through. That's workable for a few hundred emails. Why am I still not getting emails after deleting stuff?

A few usual suspects:

Trash wasn't emptied. Check the Trash folder. If it's full of what you just deleted, empty it.

Drive or Photos is the real problem. Your storage page will show it. Large videos in Photos and old backups in Drive are common culprits. Cleaning Gmail can't offset a Photos library that's larger than your whole quota.

Storage hasn't refreshed yet. Google says storage totals can take up to 24 hours to update. It's usually much faster, but give it a few hours before assuming something is broken.

You're only just under the limit. If you freed 50 MB and you're at 14.99 GB, one incoming attachment pushes you back over. Aim for at least 1 to 2 GB of headroom.

Should I buy more Google storage instead?

Buying a Google One plan fixes the problem instantly, and if you're mid-crisis that's a reasonable call. But most full Gmail accounts are full of mail nobody will ever open again: promotions from 2019, notification emails, duplicate receipts. Paying monthly to store that is optional. How do I stop my Gmail from filling up again?

Once mail is flowing, set up a little prevention so you're not back here in six months. Unsubscribe from senders you never read. Create a filter for category:promotions older_than:30d with the action Delete it so marketing email clears itself. And check your storage page every couple of months. Frequently asked questions

Will I get the emails that bounced while my Gmail was full?

No. Gmail rejects incoming mail when you're over quota, so those messages are never stored. The sender receives a bounce notice, and you'll need to ask them to send it again.

What does the sender see when my Gmail mailbox is full?

They get an automatic bounce message saying the account they tried to reach is over quota. Some people mistake this for a deleted or inactive account, so it's worth telling important contacts once you've fixed it.

How long does it take for Gmail to start receiving email after I free up space?

Usually within minutes, but Google says storage totals can take up to 24 hours to update. If mail still isn't arriving after you've emptied Trash, wait a few hours and check your storage page again.

Can I still send emails when my Gmail storage is full?

No. When your account is over its storage limit, Gmail blocks both sending and receiving until you free up space or add more storage.

Will Google delete my emails if my storage stays full?

It can. Google's policy allows it to delete content from Gmail, Drive, and Photos if an account stays over its storage limit for two years or more, after sending warnings first.
