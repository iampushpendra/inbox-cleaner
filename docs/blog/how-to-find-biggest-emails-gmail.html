How to Find the Biggest Emails in Gmail

Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: To find the biggest emails in Gmail, type larger:10M into the search bar and press Enter. Gmail has no sort-by-size button, but this operator returns every email over 10 MB across your whole account, sent mail included. Lower the number to larger:5M or larger:2M to widen the net, then delete what you don't need and empty Trash.

Gmail will happily tell you that you're at 14.7 of 15 GB. What it won't do is show you which emails are responsible. There's no size column, no sort menu, and the mobile app is even less helpful. The good news is that a couple of search operators do the job well, and the whole hunt takes about five minutes once you know the order to run them in.

How do I find the biggest emails in Gmail?

Work from the top of the size range down. Start high so the first result set is small and obviously junk, then loosen the threshold until the batches stop being worth the review time.

  1. Click into the Gmail search bar and type larger:10M, then press Enter. You'll get every email over 10 MB in the account, not just the inbox.
  2. If only a handful come back, step down: larger:5M, then larger:2M. Each pass roughly doubles or triples the count.
  3. Narrow by age or type when a batch gets too broad to skim. larger:5M older_than:1y targets old heavy mail. larger:5M has:attachment skips long text threads and shows only files.
  4. Skim the list. Download anything you actually want, then tick the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search", and hit the Trash icon.
  5. Open Trash in the left sidebar and click Empty Trash now. Until you do, the space isn't back.

The operator accepts M for megabytes and K for kilobytes, and a bare number means bytes. size:10M behaves the same as larger:10M. There's also smaller:, which is handy for the opposite job: larger:1M smaller:5M brackets the mid-range files that are individually small but collectively add up.

Why can't I just sort Gmail by size?

Because the feature doesn't exist. Gmail has never offered sort-by-size, sort-by-sender, or sort-by-anything other than date. Google's position is that search replaces sorting, and for size it mostly works, with one gap: you can't see how big an individual email is without opening it and hovering over each attachment.

You can bracket a specific message instead. If larger:20M returns it but larger:25M doesn't, it's between the two. That's usually enough precision when the goal is deciding what to delete.

One more thing to know: results include Sent mail. If you emailed a 20 MB video to a client three years ago, your copy is still sitting in Sent and counting against your quota. Add in:inbox if you only want received mail, or in:sent if you want to clear out your own outgoing attachments first, which tend to be the easiest calls.

Which search finds the emails using the most storage?

The single largest emails aren't always the biggest storage problem. An account with three 24 MB attachments and 40,000 newsletters has two very different cleanups available, and it's worth knowing which one you're facing before deleting anything.

Run these in order and note the counts:

  1. larger:10M. Anything here is worth a look individually. A dozen of these can be a full gigabyte.
  2. larger:1M has:attachment older_than:2y. Old attachments you've almost certainly already downloaded or no longer need. This is usually the biggest single win.
  3. has:attachment filename:pdf older_than:1y, then filename:mp4, filename:zip, filename:pptx. File-type searches surface the heavy categories that a plain size search mixes together.
  4. category:promotions older_than:6m. Individually tiny, but if the count is in the tens of thousands, this is the volume problem rather than the size problem.

If step four returns a bigger number than the first three combined, your storage isn't being eaten by big emails at all. It's being eaten by a few senders who've mailed you every day for years, and that's a different tool. Should I delete big emails or just the attachments?

Gmail doesn't let you strip an attachment and keep the message. It's all or nothing per email. If you need the text but not the file, the workaround is to download the attachment, then forward the email to yourself without it, and delete the original. Save that for the few threads that genuinely matter.

For most large emails, the honest question is whether you've opened the attachment in the last year. If not, and it came from someone you could ask again, delete it.

Frequently asked questions

Can you sort Gmail by size?

No. Gmail has no sort-by-size option in the web or mobile apps. The workaround is the search operator larger:, which filters to emails above a size you choose. Run it with a high threshold first and step down.

What is the largest email size Gmail allows?

Gmail caps attachments at 25 MB per email sent, and accepts incoming emails up to 50 MB. Anything larger arrives as a Google Drive link instead, which counts against Drive storage rather than Gmail.

Does larger: search include emails in Sent and Archive?

Yes. Searches run across All Mail by default, so larger:10M returns big emails you sent as well as ones you received or archived. Add in:inbox to limit results to the inbox only.

Can I see the size of an individual email in Gmail?

Not directly. Gmail shows attachment sizes when you hover over or open an attachment, but there is no per-email size column. Use narrowing searches like larger:20M to bracket how big a specific email is.

Why does the Google storage page still show the same number after I delete large emails?

Deleted emails sit in Trash for 30 days and still count against your quota. Empty Trash, then allow up to 24 hours for the figure at one.google.com/storage to update.
