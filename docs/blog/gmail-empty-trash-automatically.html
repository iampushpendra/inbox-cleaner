How to Empty Gmail Trash Automatically

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: Gmail empties your Trash automatically already — every message is permanently deleted 30 days after it lands there, with nothing for you to configure. To clear it sooner, open the Trash label and click "Empty Trash now." To enforce a shorter window than 30 days, run a small Google Apps Script on a daily trigger.

Most people searching for this expect to find a hidden setting. There isn't one — and there doesn't need to be. Gmail's Trash is self-cleaning by design. The real questions are how fast it happens, why your storage doesn't drop the moment you hit delete, and what to do if 30 days is too long to wait.

How do I empty Gmail Trash automatically?

You don't have to do anything. Gmail permanently deletes any message that has been in Trash for 30 days. That behavior is built in, applies to every account, and can't be turned off or extended.

The part that confuses people is the timing. The 30-day clock runs per message, not per folder. A message you deleted this morning and one you deleted three weeks ago expire on different days, so Trash drains continuously rather than clearing out in one dramatic sweep. If you deleted 40,000 emails last Tuesday, all 40,000 will still be sitting in Trash — and still counting against your storage quota — until that Tuesday comes around again next month.

That's the catch worth internalizing: deleting mail and freeing space are two separate events, 30 days apart. Anything in Trash and Spam counts toward your 15 GB the whole time.

How do I empty Gmail Trash right now?

If you're trying to reclaim storage today, don't wait for the automatic purge — force it:

  1. In Gmail's left sidebar, scroll down and click More, then click Trash.
  2. At the top of the message list, click Empty Trash now.
  3. Click OK in the confirmation dialog. Every message in Trash is permanently deleted and the space it used is released.
  4. Do the same in Spam — it's a separate folder with its own 30-day timer and its own share of your quota. The link there reads Delete all spam messages now.

This is genuinely permanent. There's no second Trash behind the Trash. On a personal account, nothing you empty here comes back; on Google Workspace, an admin has a 25-day recovery window from the Admin console, and that's the only safety net.

Worth clearing Spam at the same time — on a neglected account it can hold years of accumulated junk. If yours is overflowing, How to Delete Spam Emails in Gmail covers stopping the inflow as well as clearing the backlog.

Can a Gmail filter empty the Trash automatically?

No — and this is the most common dead end. A Gmail filter's Delete it action moves a message to Trash. It doesn't permanently delete it, and filters only run on incoming mail, so nothing you build will ever act on messages already sitting in Trash.

Filters are still the right tool for the step before this one: stopping junk from reaching your inbox in the first place, so there's less to delete later. A rule like from:noreply@example.com with the Delete it action routes that sender straight to Trash, where the 30-day timer handles the rest. How do I auto-empty Gmail Trash on a schedule?

If you want Trash cleared every 7 days instead of every 30, Google Apps Script is the only supported way to do it. It runs on Google's servers under your account, so nothing needs to stay open on your machine:

  1. Go to script.google.com and create a new project.
  2. Under Services, add the advanced Gmail API service. The standard GmailApp library can move mail to Trash but can't permanently delete it — you need the advanced service for that.
  3. Write a function that runs GmailApp.search('in:trash older_than:7d') and calls Gmail.Users.Threads.remove('me', thread.getId()) on each result.
  4. Open Triggers, add a time-driven trigger, and set it to run that function once a day.
  5. Authorize the script when prompted. Google will warn you it's unverified — that's expected for a script you wrote yourself.

Test it with a long window first, like older_than:25d, and check the execution log before tightening it. A script that permanently deletes mail deserves one careful dry run.

Why is my Gmail storage still full after emptying the Trash?

Three usual reasons. First, the quota display lags — the space is freed immediately but the usage counter can take hours to reflect it. Second, Spam wasn't cleared; it's counted separately and holds its own 30 days of mail. Third, and most often, Trash simply wasn't where your storage was going. A few thousand text-only emails take up almost nothing. A hundred emails with 20 MB attachments take up gigabytes.

If emptying Trash barely moved the needle, the problem is upstream — attachments, or a handful of senders quietly generating thousands of messages a year. How to Free Up Gmail Storage Fast covers finding the actual heavy hitters instead of guessing.

One thing to keep in mind if you're doing a large cleanup: Inbox Cleaner, Gmail's own delete button, and Gmail filters all move mail to Trash rather than erasing it. That's a feature — you get 30 days to notice a mistake. But it means a bulk delete and a storage recovery are two different steps. Clear the inbox first, confirm nothing important got caught, then empty the Trash.

Frequently asked questions

How long do emails stay in the Gmail Trash?

30 days from the moment each message was moved there. The clock runs per message, not per folder, so Trash empties itself gradually rather than all at once.

Does emptying the Trash free up Gmail storage immediately?

The space is released right away, but the storage page can lag. If your quota still looks unchanged, give it a few hours — Google's usage counter sometimes takes up to 24 hours to catch up.

Can I recover an email after the Trash has been emptied?

On a personal Gmail account, no — once it leaves Trash it's gone. On Google Workspace, an admin can restore deleted mail from the Admin console for up to 25 days after it left Trash.

Can I empty Gmail Trash from the mobile app?

Yes. Tap the menu icon, scroll to Trash, and tap "Empty trash now" at the top of the list. It permanently deletes the same messages the desktop version does.

Does the Gmail Trash count against my 15 GB of storage?

Yes. Messages in Trash and Spam both count toward your quota until they're permanently deleted, which is why deleting a huge batch of mail doesn't free space right away.
