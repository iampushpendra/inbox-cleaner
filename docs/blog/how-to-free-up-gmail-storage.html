How to Free Up Gmail Storage Fast

Updated June 2026 · 5 min read

Google gives you 15 GB of free storage shared across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos. When that fills up, Gmail stops accepting new emails. Here's how to reclaim space fast — without accidentally deleting anything important.

Why is Gmail storage filling up?

Most storage in Gmail is consumed by a small number of senders: newsletters, promotional emails, social notifications, and automated alerts. These pile up quietly over years. A sender sending you one email a day for three years = over 1,000 emails. Multiply that by a dozen senders and you're looking at 10,000+ emails you've never opened.

Attachments are the other culprit. Emails with large attachments (PDFs, images, videos) eat storage fast even if there are only a few dozen of them.

Step 1: Find what's taking up space

Start at one.google.com/storage to see how your 15 GB breaks down across Gmail, Drive, and Photos. If Gmail is the main offender, proceed below.

In Gmail, search for large emails first: type has:attachment larger:10mb in the search bar. These are your biggest individual space-wasters. Delete any that aren't important — especially old files you've already downloaded.

Step 2: Delete bulk emails by sender

Large attachments are easy to find but rarely account for the bulk of storage. The real volume comes from high-frequency senders. The fastest way to find them:

  1. Go to inbox-cleaner.vercel.app and click Connect Gmail.
  2. The tool scans your inbox and ranks every sender by email count — highest first. This immediately shows you who is taking up the most space.
  3. Check the boxes next to the top offenders: newsletters, promo emails, job alert services, social notifications.
  4. Click Move to Trash. All their emails move at once. You can do multiple senders in one operation.

For most people, deleting the top 5–10 senders recovers thousands of emails and several gigabytes in a few minutes.

Step 3: Empty Trash

Emails in Trash still count toward your storage. After moving emails to Trash, go to Gmail → Trash → Empty Trash now to free the storage immediately. Be sure you don't need to restore anything first — emptying Trash is permanent.

Step 4: Clear Spam

Gmail's Spam folder counts toward storage too. Go to Spam → Delete all spam messages to clear it.

Step 5: Handle Google Drive and Photos

If Gmail isn't the main offender, check Drive and Photos. In Drive, sort by Storage used (right-click any folder → "Sort by storage used") to find large files. In Google Photos, look for videos — they're the biggest storage consumers.

What if I'm still over the limit after deleting?

It can take up to 24 hours for Google's storage counter to update after bulk deletions. If you've emptied Trash and Spam and still see high usage, wait a day and check again. If you're consistently hitting the 15 GB limit, the long-term solution is either regular cleanup (monthly is enough for most people) or upgrading to Google One for more storage.

How do I stop Gmail storage from filling up again?

The practical approach: unsubscribe from newsletters you never read, and run a bulk cleanup every 1–2 months before you hit the limit again. Setting a calendar reminder for "Gmail cleanup" takes 5 minutes and saves you the "storage full" panic.
