How to Delete Twitter (X) Notification Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete Twitter (X) notification emails in Gmail, search from:(@x.com OR @twitter.com) -is:starred, tick the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit Trash. You need both domains because years of old alerts came from twitter.com and newer ones come from x.com. Then turn off email notifications in X's settings so they stop arriving.

Twitter became X in 2023, but your inbox never got the memo. If your account is more than a few years old, Gmail is holding a long trail of "you have new notifications" nudges, follower alerts, highlight digests, login codes, and "people you may know" suggestions. Half of them came from twitter.com. The newer ones come from x.com. None of them are worth keeping, because everything they point to still lives on X.

How do I delete Twitter (X) notification emails in Gmail?

The trick is to search both domains at once. A search for only from:x.com misses everything sent before the rebrand, and a search for only twitter.com misses everything since. Here is the full process:

  1. In Gmail's search bar, type from:(@x.com OR @twitter.com) -is:starred and press Enter. The -is:starred part protects anything you deliberately saved.
  2. Tick the checkbox at the top-left of the message list. That selects the 50 emails on the current page.
  3. A banner appears: "Select all conversations that match this search." Click it. The selection now covers every matching Twitter and X email.
  4. Click the Trash icon. Everything moves to Trash and stays recoverable for 30 days.
  5. Open Trash in the sidebar and click Empty Trash now when you're sure. Trashed emails still count against your 15 GB until they're permanently removed.

Which email addresses does X send notifications from?

Sometimes you want to keep one kind of email, like security alerts, and clear the rest. In that case, target addresses one at a time. These are the main senders you'll find in an older inbox:

  • notify@x.com and notify@twitter.com: likes, replies, mentions, new followers, and "you have unread notifications" reminders.
  • info@x.com and info@twitter.com: highlight digests, "top posts for you," recommended accounts, and product news.
  • verify@x.com and verify@twitter.com: login codes, email confirmations, and password resets.
  • premium@x.com and similar: subscription receipts and Premium (formerly Twitter Blue) billing.

Gmail operators stack, so you can be precise. To clear everything except verification mail, search from:(@x.com OR @twitter.com) -from:verify. To clear only old mail and keep the last month, add older_than:1m. To clear expired login codes, search from:(verify@x.com OR verify@twitter.com) older_than:7d. A week-old login code can't be used by anyone.

How do I stop X from sending me notification emails?

Clearing the backlog only fixes the past. X will keep emailing until you change its settings:

  1. On X, open More (or your profile menu on mobile), then Settings and privacy.
  2. Click Notifications, then Preferences, then Email notifications.
  3. Switch the main toggle off. That stops activity alerts, digests, and recommendations in one go.
  4. If you'd rather keep a few, leave the toggle on and untick categories like "Top posts and stories," "Updates about the performance of your posts," and "News about X product and feature updates."
  5. Security emails like login codes and password resets keep arriving either way. That's intentional, and it's a good thing.

Any X marketing email also has an Unsubscribe link at the bottom. For senders that ignore unsubscribe requests, How to Unsubscribe from Emails in Gmail walks through Gmail's built-in options.

Can Gmail automatically delete X emails?

Yes. If you still want some alerts but don't want them piling up, let a filter clean them on a delay. Search from:(@x.com OR @twitter.com) -from:verify older_than:7d, click the search options icon at the right of the search bar, choose Create filter, and tick Delete it. Also tick Also apply filter to matching conversations to clear the existing pile in the same step.

New alerts stay visible for a week, which is plenty of time to reply to a mention. After that, Gmail clears them. For more recipes and how to avoid deleting the wrong thing, see Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Does deleting X emails in Gmail affect my X account?

No. The emails are copies of alerts. Your posts, followers, DMs, and in-app notifications stay on X, so clearing them from Gmail changes nothing on your account.

Why do I still get emails from twitter.com after the rebrand to X?

Older emails keep the twitter.com sender they were sent with, and some X systems still used twitter.com addresses during the transition. That is why your search should cover both @x.com and @twitter.com.

Is it safe to delete X login code and verification emails?

Yes, once they are a few days old. Login codes and verification links expire quickly. Keep only recent alerts about password changes or new logins until you have confirmed they were you.

How do I stop X emails if I deleted my account?

A deactivated account is permanently deleted after 30 days, and emails stop then. If they continue, open any X email, click Unsubscribe at the bottom, or create a Gmail filter that deletes mail from @x.com and @twitter.com.

Can I recover X emails I deleted by mistake?

Yes, for 30 days. Open Trash in Gmail, select the emails, and click Move to Inbox. After 30 days, or once you empty Trash, they are gone for good.
