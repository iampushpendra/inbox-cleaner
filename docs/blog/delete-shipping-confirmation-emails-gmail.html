How to Delete Shipping Confirmation Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete shipping confirmation emails in Gmail, search subject:(shipped OR "on its way" OR "out for delivery" OR delivered OR "tracking number") older_than:30d, click the top checkbox, choose "Select all conversations that match this search," and click Trash. The older_than:30d part keeps packages still in transit. Run a second search for carrier senders like UPS, FedEx, and USPS to catch the rest.

One online order can send you five emails. "Order confirmed." "Your order has shipped." "Out for delivery." "Delivered." Then the carrier sends its own versions of the same news. Multiply that by every store you buy from, and shipping notices quietly become one of the biggest piles in your inbox.

They're useful for about three days. After the box is on your porch, they're clutter. The tricky part is that they come from hundreds of different senders, so you can't just delete one address. You need a search that matches what the emails say, not who sent them.

How do I delete shipping confirmation emails in Gmail?

Search by subject line. Almost every store uses the same handful of phrases, so one search catches most of them.

  1. In the Gmail search bar, paste subject:(shipped OR "on its way" OR "out for delivery" OR delivered OR "tracking number") older_than:30d and press Enter.
  2. Scroll the results. Star anything you still need, like an expensive purchase or an open return. Then add -is:starred to the end of the search and run it again.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top of the message list. Gmail selects the 50 emails on screen.
  4. Click the banner that says "Select all conversations that match this search" to extend the selection to every match.
  5. Click the Trash icon. Everything moves to Trash, where it stays recoverable for 30 days.

Then run a second pass for the carriers: from:(ups.com OR fedex.com OR usps.com OR dhl.com) older_than:30d. Carrier alerts often use subjects like "UPS Update" or "Your Delivery Is Scheduled," which the first search misses. If you want to tune these searches further, Gmail Search Operators to Find Old Emails covers every operator worth knowing.

Which shipping emails should I keep?

Most shipping emails are copies of information the store already keeps. Your order history, tracking, and invoices live in your account on the store's website. Still, a few are worth a second look before you delete:

  • Packages still in transit. The older_than:30d filter handles this. Shorten it to older_than:14d if you want to be more aggressive.
  • Open returns. Return labels and drop-off QR codes often only exist in the email. Search return label first and star anything active.
  • Big-ticket purchases. Electronics and appliances may need proof of purchase for warranty claims. The shipping email isn't usually the receipt, but check that you have the order confirmation somewhere.
  • Small shops without accounts. If you checked out as a guest, the email may be your only record. Keep the order confirmation, delete the shipping updates.

One easy rule: keep receipts, delete status updates. "Shipped" and "delivered" notices never carry information you can't get elsewhere.

Why doesn't my search catch every shipping email?

Stores word things differently. Some say "Your package is headed your way." Others use "Dispatched" (common in the UK) or put the order number in the subject with no shipping word at all. Add phrases you see in your own inbox: subject:(shipped OR dispatched OR "headed your way" OR "has been delivered").

You can also use Gmail's built-in category. Gmail tags many order and shipping emails as Updates, so category:updates older_than:30d pulls them in too. Be careful, though. The Updates tab also holds bills, statements, and account alerts, so review before you hit Trash.

For your highest-volume stores, deleting by sender is cleaner than guessing subject lines. How do I stop shipping emails from piling up again?

You can't stop them entirely. Stores send them whether you want them or not. But you can keep them out of your way.

Turn off carrier emails. UPS My Choice, FedEx Delivery Manager, and USPS Informed Delivery all let you switch email alerts off or move them to app notifications. If you already get the store's shipping email, the carrier's copy is pure duplication.

Auto-archive with a filter. Search from:(ups.com OR fedex.com OR usps.com), click the filter icon in the search bar, and choose Create filter. Pick Skip the Inbox so they're archived on arrival but still searchable if a delivery goes missing. For more recipes, see Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

Schedule a monthly sweep. Filters only act on new mail, and they can't wait until a package arrives. Save your shipping search somewhere handy and re-run it once a month. It takes under a minute.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to delete shipping confirmation emails?

Yes, once the package has arrived and the return window has closed. The store keeps your order history in your account, so the shipping email is only a copy. Keep the order confirmation or receipt if you need proof of purchase.

Can I still track a package after deleting the shipping email?

Usually yes. Log in to the store's website and open the order, or use the carrier's app if you saved the tracking number. That's why the safe search adds older_than:30d, so anything still in transit stays in your inbox.

Why does Gmail show a package tracker above my shipping emails?

Gmail reads tracking numbers from order emails and shows delivery status in the inbox. If you delete the email, that tracker card goes away too, so wait until the package is delivered.

How do I stop UPS and FedEx from emailing me about every package?

In UPS My Choice or FedEx Delivery Manager, open notification settings and turn off email alerts, or switch them to app notifications. USPS Informed Delivery lets you turn off the daily digest the same way.

Will deleting shipping emails free up much Gmail storage?

A little. Each one is small, but they're image-heavy and arrive several per order, so a few thousand can add up to tens of megabytes. The bigger win is a cleaner inbox and easier searches.
