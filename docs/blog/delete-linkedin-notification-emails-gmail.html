How to Delete LinkedIn Notification Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete LinkedIn notification emails in Gmail, search from:linkedin.com -is:starred, tick the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit Trash. That one search covers every LinkedIn address: job alerts, invitations, message digests, and "you appeared in searches." Then turn off the email categories in LinkedIn's settings so the pile doesn't rebuild.

LinkedIn is usually the heaviest emailer in a professional inbox. It sends from many addresses, and each one has its own schedule: daily job alerts, weekly "who viewed your profile" digests, a nudge every time a connection posts, and reminders about invitations you ignored months ago. A few years of that adds up to thousands of emails, and none of them are worth keeping. The real conversations live inside LinkedIn, not in Gmail.

How do I delete LinkedIn notification emails in Gmail?

Every LinkedIn sending address ends in linkedin.com, so one search catches all of them:

  1. In Gmail's search bar, type from:linkedin.com -is:starred and press Enter. The -is:starred part keeps anything you flagged, like a recruiter thread, out of the selection.
  2. Tick the checkbox at the top-left of the message list. That selects the 50 emails on the current page.
  3. A banner appears: "Select all conversations that match this search." Click it. The selection now covers every LinkedIn email, not just the visible page.
  4. Click the Trash icon. Everything moves to Trash and stays recoverable for 30 days.
  5. Open Trash in the sidebar and click Empty Trash now once you're sure. Until then, those emails still count against your 15 GB.

Which LinkedIn email addresses send notifications?

Sometimes you want to clear the noise but keep one type of LinkedIn email, such as message notifications from recruiters. In that case, target the addresses one at a time. These are the main senders:

  • jobalerts-noreply@linkedin.com: saved job searches and "jobs you may be interested in."
  • notifications-noreply@linkedin.com: profile views, post reactions, "you appeared in 12 searches this week."
  • invitations@linkedin.com: connection requests and the reminders that follow them.
  • messages-noreply@linkedin.com and messaging-digest-noreply@linkedin.com: alerts that someone sent you a LinkedIn message.
  • e.linkedin.com subdomains: LinkedIn Premium offers and other marketing.

To delete everything except message alerts, search from:linkedin.com -from:messages-noreply@linkedin.com -is:starred. To keep the last three months and clear everything older, add older_than:3m. Gmail's search operators stack, so you can narrow the selection as much as you need before you hit Trash.

How do I stop LinkedIn from sending so many emails?

Deleting the backlog only fixes the past. LinkedIn will refill your inbox within a few weeks unless you change its email settings, and they're spread out more than you'd expect:

  1. On LinkedIn, click your profile photo, then Settings & Privacy.
  2. Open the Notifications section and choose Email. LinkedIn has moved this menu around over the years, so search settings for "email" if you can't find it.
  3. Work through each category, including Job alerts, Invitations, Network updates, Posting and commenting, and Searching for you, and switch off the ones you don't need.
  4. Open any LinkedIn marketing email and click Unsubscribe at the bottom to stop Premium offers. Those are controlled separately from notifications.

LinkedIn won't let you turn off security and account emails, such as sign-in alerts and password resets. That's fine. They're rare, and you want those anyway. For a wider look at cutting off senders at the source, see How to Unsubscribe from Emails in Gmail.

Can Gmail automatically delete LinkedIn emails?

Yes. If you want to keep some LinkedIn alerts but don't want them piling up, a filter can clear them on a delay. In Gmail's search bar, type from:linkedin.com older_than:7d, click the search options icon, choose Create filter, and tick Delete it. Also tick Also apply filter to matching conversations to clear the existing backlog at the same time.

New alerts still reach your inbox for a week, which gives you time to act on a job alert or accept an invitation. After that, they clear themselves out. For more filter recipes, including how to stack conditions and avoid deleting the wrong thing, see Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Will deleting LinkedIn emails in Gmail delete my LinkedIn messages?

No. LinkedIn emails are copies of alerts. Your messages, connections, and notifications stay inside LinkedIn itself, so deleting the emails in Gmail changes nothing on your LinkedIn account.

Can I keep LinkedIn messages but delete job alerts?

Yes. Search from:jobalerts-noreply@linkedin.com to target only job alert emails, or use from:linkedin.com -from:messages-noreply@linkedin.com to delete everything except message notifications.

Why do I still get LinkedIn emails after turning off notifications?

LinkedIn splits email into many separate categories, and switching off one does not switch off the rest. Marketing mail and security or account emails are also controlled separately, and account emails cannot be turned off at all.

How do I automatically delete LinkedIn emails in Gmail?

Search from:linkedin.com older_than:7d, open the search options, click Create filter, and tick Delete it. New LinkedIn emails will still arrive, but Gmail clears them once they are a week old.

Can I get deleted LinkedIn emails back?

Yes, for 30 days. Deleted emails sit in Gmail's Trash folder until Gmail removes them permanently after 30 days, or until you empty Trash yourself. Select them in Trash and click Move to Inbox to restore them.
