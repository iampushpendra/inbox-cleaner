How to Delete Emails from Multiple Senders in Gmail at Once

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete emails from multiple senders in Gmail at once, search from:(one@example.com OR two@example.com OR three@example.com), click the top checkbox, then "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit Trash. That clears every listed sender in one pass. For more than a handful of senders, Inbox Cleaner ranks every sender in your inbox by volume so you can tick them off and delete them together — no search string to type.

Most inbox clutter comes from a small group of repeat offenders: a retailer you bought from once, two newsletters you stopped reading, a notification bot from an app you deleted. Clearing them one at a time works, but you end up running the same search-select-delete cycle over and over. Gmail's search syntax lets you do all of them in a single sweep instead.

How do I delete emails from multiple senders in Gmail at once?

The key is the OR operator, which tells Gmail to match any of the senders you list rather than all of them.

  1. Write down the addresses you want cleared. If you're not sure of the exact address, open one of their emails and copy it from the sender line.
  2. In the Gmail search bar, type from:(one@example.com OR two@example.com OR three@example.com). The parentheses group the senders; OR must be capitalized.
  3. Press Enter and skim the results. This is your last checkpoint before deleting — confirm nothing important is mixed in.
  4. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the message list to select the visible page.
  5. Click the banner that reads "Select all conversations that match this search" to extend the selection past the first 50.
  6. Click the Trash icon and wait for Gmail to confirm before starting another search.

Gmail also accepts a shorthand: from:{one@example.com two@example.com}. Curly braces mean "any of these," so it behaves identically to OR and saves some typing. Either form works with the rest of Gmail's operators, which is where this gets genuinely useful.

Can I combine multiple senders with other Gmail search filters?

Yes, and it's usually the safer way to do it. Adding a second condition narrows the batch so a broad sender list can't sweep up something you meant to keep:

  • from:(a@x.com OR b@y.com) older_than:1y — clears the old backlog from those senders, leaves recent mail alone.
  • from:(a@x.com OR b@y.com) has:attachment larger:5M — targets only the storage-heavy messages.
  • from:(@deals.example.com OR @mail.example.net) — matches whole domains, useful for companies that rotate sending addresses.
  • from:(a@x.com OR b@y.com) -is:starred — excludes anything you flagged as important.

That last one is worth building into the habit. Star the few messages you'd hate to lose, then exclude starred mail from every bulk delete you run. If you only ever need to clear one address at a time, How to Delete All Emails from One Sender in Gmail covers the simpler single-sender version of this workflow.

Why does the OR search stop working with a long list of senders?

Two things break down as the list grows. First, the search string gets fragile — one mistyped address silently drops that sender from the results, and you won't notice until their emails are still sitting there afterward. Second, the underlying limits haven't changed: Gmail still renders 50 conversations per page, and the "select all conversations that match this search" banner still stalls when a single search matches tens of thousands of messages. Bundling ten senders into one query makes that match bigger, not smaller, so you hit the timeout sooner than you would clearing them individually. The mechanics behind that cap are explained in How to Select All Emails in Gmail.

The practical workaround is batching: group three or four senders per search, delete, then run the next group. It works, but it turns a two-minute job into a fifteen-minute one.

What's the fastest way to clear a long list of senders?

Skip the search string. Inbox Cleaner scans your entire inbox through the Gmail API and ranks every sender by how many emails they've sent you, so you select from a list instead of typing addresses:

  1. Go to inbox-cleaner.vercel.app and click Connect Gmail.
  2. Wait for the scan. Every sender appears ranked by email count, highest first.
  3. Tick the checkbox next to each sender you want gone — five, twenty, however many.
  4. Click Move to Trash. All of them clear at once, in a single action.

The ranking matters as much as the bulk delete. Most people can't name the senders filling their inbox — the list surfaces them, and the top ten usually account for the majority of the clutter. Will deleting multiple senders at once remove anything I need?

It can, if you're careless with the list — the risk scales with how many senders you bundle together. Three habits keep it safe. Read the result count before deleting; if a search for four senders returns 40,000 messages, something matched more broadly than you expected. Exclude starred mail with -is:starred. And remember that nothing is permanently gone: everything lands in Trash and stays there 30 days, so search in:trash from:sender and restore if you overshoot.

Deleting the backlog also doesn't stop new mail from the same senders. If they're newsletters or marketing lists, unsubscribe as you go — otherwise you'll be running the same OR search again in three months.

Frequently asked questions

How many senders can I put in one Gmail search?

Gmail doesn't publish a hard limit, but the search bar becomes impractical well before it breaks — around eight to ten addresses, a single typo is hard to spot and the query is hard to reuse. Split long lists into several searches, or use a tool that handles sender selection with checkboxes.

Does the OR operator have to be capitalized in Gmail search?

Yes. Gmail treats a lowercase "or" as an ordinary search word, so from:(a@x.com or b@y.com) will not work as intended. Capitalize OR, or use the curly-brace shorthand from:{a@x.com b@y.com}, which means the same thing.

Can I delete emails from a whole domain instead of listing every address?

Yes. Search from:(@newsletters.example.com) to match every sender at that domain. This is useful for companies that send from dozens of rotating addresses like noreply-1@, deals@, and updates@.

Can I combine multiple senders with a date range in one search?

Yes, and it's a good safety habit. from:(a@x.com OR b@y.com) older_than:1y keeps recent mail from those senders intact while clearing the old backlog, which reduces the chance of deleting something you still need.

If I delete emails from multiple senders, can I get them back?

Yes, for 30 days. Gmail moves deleted mail to Trash and only purges it after that window, so you can search within Trash and restore anything you removed by mistake.
