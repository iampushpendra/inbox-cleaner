Gmail Storage Almost Full? What to Do

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: When Gmail says storage is almost full, check one.google.com/storage first to see whether Gmail, Drive or Photos is the culprit — the 15 GB is shared. If it's Gmail, delete the heaviest mail first with the search larger:10M, clear old promotional and social mail, then empty Trash and Spam so the space actually comes back. Fifteen minutes of that usually buys back a year.

The "almost full" warning is the good version of this problem. Nothing is broken yet — mail is still arriving, Drive still accepts uploads, and you can clean up deliberately instead of scrambling. That changes at 100%, when Gmail starts bouncing incoming mail back to whoever sent it. Treat the warning as a deadline with slack in it, not an emergency.

What should I do when my Gmail storage is almost full?

Work in this order. Each pass is bigger than the one before it, and most people never need to get past step three.

  1. Open one.google.com/storage and read the breakdown. It splits your 15 GB across Gmail, Drive and Google Photos. If Photos is holding 9 GB and Gmail only 2 GB, deleting emails is the wrong job.
  2. Search larger:10M in Gmail. These are the attachment-heavy emails — decks, video files, scanned PDFs. A few hundred of them routinely outweigh 40,000 plain text messages.
  3. Run category:promotions older_than:6m and category:social older_than:6m and delete both batches. This is the bulk of most inboxes by count.
  4. Open Trash in the left sidebar and click Empty Trash now, then do the same in Spam. Skipping this is the single most common reason people delete thousands of emails and watch the number refuse to move.
  5. Recheck the storage page. Give it a few minutes — Google's figure lags the actual deletion.

Add -is:starred to any broad search before you delete. It costs nothing and it excludes anything you deliberately flagged as worth keeping.

How do I find out what's actually using my Gmail storage?

Size and volume are different problems. Sort by size and you're hunting a handful of large attachments. Sort by sender and you're hunting the few senders who've mailed you daily for six years.

For size, the search bar does the work: larger:10M, then larger:5M, then has:attachment older_than:2y. For volume, Gmail gives you nothing useful — there's no "show me my top senders" view, so you end up guessing at names you happen to remember. That's the gap worth closing, because the senders filling your mailbox are almost never the ones you'd think of first. Why is my storage still almost full after deleting emails?

Three reasons, in order of likelihood.

First, you didn't empty Trash. Deleted mail sits there for 30 days and counts against your quota the whole time. Second, the number is stale — Google says it can take up to 24 hours to update. Third, you deleted by count instead of by size: clearing 20,000 newsletters feels productive and might free 200 MB, while ten forgotten video attachments free more.

If you've already crossed into fully full and mail is bouncing, the recovery order is slightly different — Gmail Storage Full? How to Fix It covers what to do when Gmail has already stopped accepting messages.

How do I stop Gmail storage from filling up again?

A one-off cleanup buys you a year or two. A filter buys you the rest of the account's life, and takes about a minute to set up.

Type category:promotions older_than:30d into the Gmail search bar, click the dropdown arrow at the right of the bar, choose Create filter, then tick Delete it. Marketing mail now clears itself after 30 days — long enough that a discount code or order confirmation is still there when you need it. Repeat with category:social older_than:30d for notification mail from social platforms. Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically walks through the other conditions worth automating.

The other half is reducing inflow. Unsubscribing from a dozen lists you never open removes more future mail than any filter. Then set a yearly reminder to run larger:10M — large attachments quietly rebuild while everything else stays tidy. If you'd rather sweep by age than by category, How to Delete Old Emails in Gmail covers the date-range approach.

Should I just buy more storage instead?

Sometimes, yes. Google One starts at around $1.99/month for 100 GB, and if your mailbox is genuinely full of work you need — years of client correspondence, project archives, legal records — paying is faster and safer than picking through it.

But for most people hitting the warning for the first time, the 15 GB isn't full of anything they'd miss — it's eight years of receipts, shipping notifications and newsletters from companies that no longer exist. Upgrading there buys time without fixing the cause, and you hit the same wall at 100 GB with far more to sort through. Clean first, decide after.

Frequently asked questions

At what percentage does Gmail warn you that storage is almost full?

Google starts showing warnings in Gmail and Drive once you pass roughly 80% of your 15 GB, and the alerts get more prominent above 90%. Nothing breaks at that point — the warning is a heads-up, not a cutoff.

What happens if I ignore the Gmail storage warning?

Once you actually hit 100%, Gmail stops receiving new mail and senders get a bounce message, and you can't upload to Drive or back up to Photos. Incoming mail sent while you're full is not queued for later delivery.

How long does it take for Gmail storage to update after deleting emails?

Usually minutes, but Google says it can take up to 24 hours. If the number hasn't moved, first confirm you emptied Trash — deleted mail keeps counting against your quota for 30 days otherwise.

Do emails in Spam and Trash count toward Gmail storage?

Yes. Both folders count against your 15 GB until Gmail auto-purges them after 30 days. Emptying them manually is one of the fastest ways to get space back with no risk to your inbox.

Does deleting Google Photos or Drive files free up Gmail storage?

Yes — the 15 GB is a single shared pool across Gmail, Drive and Photos, so space freed anywhere is available everywhere. Check the breakdown at one.google.com/storage before you decide which service to clean.
