Gmail Search Operators to Find Old Emails

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: The Gmail search operators to find old emails are older_than: for a rolling cutoff (older_than:1y, older_than:6m, older_than:30d) and before: / after: for fixed dates in YYYY/MM/DD format (before:2024/01/01). Stack them with from:, larger:, has:attachment, or category:promotions to narrow the results, and put a minus sign in front of anything you want to exclude.

Gmail's search bar is far more capable than it looks. It has no "sort by oldest" button, but a handful of operators let you pull up exactly the slice of old mail you care about — a single sender from 2019, every attachment over 10 MB from before last year, or promotions you never opened. Here's the cheat sheet, and how to combine the pieces.

What are the Gmail search operators to find old emails?

Four operators handle dates. Everything else in this post is a modifier you stack on top of them.

  • older_than: — messages older than a relative period. Units are d (days), m (months), and y (years). older_than:2y means "more than two years ago, counted from today."
  • newer_than: — the reverse. newer_than:7d is the last week. Useful for bracketing a range with older_than:.
  • before: — messages received before a fixed date. before:2023/01/01 returns everything up to the end of 2022.
  • after: — messages received on or after a fixed date. Pair it with before: to get an exact window.

The key difference: older_than: is a rolling window that shifts forward every day, while before: is a line that never moves. Use the rolling version for quick one-off searches and the fixed version when you're working through a cleanup over several sessions and need the same results each time.

How do I combine Gmail search operators to narrow down old emails?

Gmail treats a space between operators as AND, so every term you add shrinks the results. Build the search in this order:

  1. Start with an age operator: older_than:2y for a rolling cutoff, or before:2024/01/01 for a fixed one.
  2. Add a source. from:linkedin.com, category:promotions, category:social, or label:receipts each focus the search on one kind of mail.
  3. Add a size or attachment filter if storage is the goal: larger:5M or has:attachment.
  4. Exclude what you want to keep with a minus sign: -is:starred -is:important -from:bank.com.
  5. Press Enter, check the first page and the result count, then decide whether to archive, label, or trash the results.

A finished search might look like older_than:1y category:promotions -is:starred — every promotional email over a year old that you didn't star. If that's what you're after, How to Delete Emails Older Than a Year in Gmail covers the select-all and delete steps in detail.

Which modifiers work best with date operators?

These are the ones that earn their place in a cleanup search:

  • is:unread — mail you never opened. is:unread older_than:6m is almost always safe to clear.
  • larger:10M / smaller:1M — filter by message size. Accepts K and M suffixes.
  • has:attachment and filename:pdf — find old files, or a specific type of file.
  • in:inbox, in:sent, in:anywhere — control where Gmail looks. By default, search skips Spam and Trash; in:anywhere includes them.
  • {from:a.com from:b.com} — curly braces mean OR. You can also type OR in capitals between terms.
  • "exact phrase" — quotes match a phrase exactly, like "order confirmation" before:2022/01/01.
  • - — a leading minus negates any operator, including dates: -older_than:30d is the same as newer_than:30d.

If your real goal is reclaiming space, size beats age. How to Find the Biggest Emails in Gmail shows how to use larger: to find the handful of messages that account for most of your quota.

How do I search Gmail for emails between two dates?

Combine after: and before:. For the whole of 2023, search after:2022/12/31 before:2024/01/01. Gmail interprets these dates as midnight, so setting the edges one day outside your target range avoids losing messages at the boundaries.

For a relative range, bracket with older_than: and newer_than:: older_than:1y newer_than:2y returns mail between one and two years old. This is handy for working backward through a backlog one year at a time — each slice is small enough for Gmail's select-all to finish without stalling.

If you need precision down to the hour, before: and after: also accept a Unix timestamp in seconds, such as after:1704067200. Most people never need this, but it helps when time zones push messages to the wrong side of a date boundary.

What if the search finds tens of thousands of old emails?

Finding them is the easy part. Acting on them is where Gmail struggles. The page checkbox selects only 50 conversations, and the "Select all conversations that match this search" banner that extends it often stalls or partially completes when the result count runs into five figures.

Two things help. First, split the search into smaller date windows and work through them one by one. Second, look at who sent the mail. Can I save a Gmail search for old emails?

Gmail has no saved-search button, but there are two workarounds. Bookmark the results page — the search is encoded in the URL, so the bookmark reruns it. Or open the search options panel (the sliders icon in the search bar) and click Create filter to turn the search into a filter. Just remember that filters only act on new incoming mail, so a filter with older_than: in it will never match anything.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Gmail search to find my oldest emails?

Search older_than:10y to see anything from over a decade ago, or before:2015/01/01 for a fixed cutoff. Gmail has no sort-by-oldest button, but you can jump to the last page of results by clicking the older arrow repeatedly or narrowing the date range until the oldest messages appear.

What date format does Gmail search use?

Gmail's before: and after: operators use YYYY/MM/DD, such as before:2024/06/30. Gmail also accepts MM/DD/YYYY and a Unix timestamp in seconds, which is useful when you need an exact time rather than midnight.

Does Gmail search include Spam and Trash?

No. Regular searches skip Spam and Trash. Add in:anywhere to include them, or in:trash or in:spam to search only those folders.

Can I search Gmail for old unread emails only?

Yes. Combine is:unread with an age operator, for example is:unread older_than:6m. That surfaces mail you never opened and probably never will, which is usually safe to archive or delete.

Why does my older_than search return different results each day?

older_than: counts back from the current date, so the cutoff moves forward every day. Use before: with a fixed date if you need the same result set across several cleanup sessions.
