How to Delete Facebook Notification Emails in Gmail

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To delete Facebook notification emails in Gmail, search from:facebookmail.com -is:starred, tick the top checkbox, click "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit Trash. Almost every Facebook alert, from friend requests to group posts to birthday reminders, comes from a facebookmail.com address. Then switch email notifications off in Facebook's settings so the pile doesn't come back.

If you opened a Facebook account more than a few years ago, it has probably been emailing you the whole time. Friend requests. Comments on posts you barely remember. Group updates. "You have 14 new notifications." Birthday reminders for people you haven't spoken to since school. Each email is small, but there are thousands of them, and they bury the email you actually need. Nothing in them is worth keeping, because everything they point to still lives on Facebook.

How do I delete Facebook notification emails in Gmail?

Facebook sends notifications from addresses ending in facebookmail.com, so one search catches nearly all of them. This version leaves security alerts out so you can check them first:

  1. In Gmail's search bar, type from:facebookmail.com -from:security@facebookmail.com -is:starred and press Enter.
  2. Tick the checkbox at the top-left of the message list. That selects the 50 emails on the current page.
  3. A banner appears: "Select all conversations that match this search." Click it. The selection now covers every matching Facebook email, not just the visible page.
  4. Click the Trash icon. Everything moves to Trash and stays recoverable for 30 days.
  5. Run a second search for from:facebook.com OR from:meta.com -is:starred to catch ads, marketing, and Meta account emails that skip facebookmail.com. Trash those the same way.

Trashed emails still count against your 15 GB until they're gone for good. Open Trash in the sidebar and click Empty Trash now once you're sure. Which Facebook email addresses send notifications?

You may want to clear most of the noise but keep one type, like group updates from a neighborhood group or event invites. In that case, target one address at a time. These are the main senders:

  • notification@facebookmail.com: comments, reactions, tags, and "you have new notifications" summaries.
  • friends@facebookmail.com: friend requests and "people you may know" suggestions.
  • groupupdates@facebookmail.com: posts and activity in groups you've joined.
  • reminders@facebookmail.com: birthdays, memories, and event reminders.
  • security@facebookmail.com: login codes, new-device alerts, and password resets.
  • facebook.com and meta.com addresses: ad receipts, marketing, and Meta account notices.

Gmail's search operators stack. To clear everything except group updates, search from:facebookmail.com -from:groupupdates@facebookmail.com. To keep the last month and clear everything older, add older_than:1m. To clear old security emails too, search from:security@facebookmail.com older_than:3m, since a three-month-old login code is no use to anyone.

What if Gmail won't delete all my Facebook emails at once?

The "select all conversations" banner handles a few thousand emails without trouble. Past that, it often stalls, deletes only part of the selection, or times out. Then you're back to 50 per page, and 10,000 Facebook emails means 200 rounds of select and delete.

Gmail also never shows how much of your inbox Facebook takes up compared with everyone else. You only find out by searching each sender yourself.

How do I stop Facebook from sending notification emails?

Deleting the backlog only fixes the past. Facebook will refill your inbox within weeks unless you change its email settings:

  1. On Facebook, click your profile photo, then Settings & privacy, then Settings.
  2. Open Notifications. Facebook moves this menu every so often, so search settings for "notifications" if you can't find it.
  3. Scroll to How you get notifications and click Email.
  4. Choose Only notifications about your account, security and privacy. That stops comments, friend suggestions, group posts, and reminders but keeps the security emails you actually want.
  5. Open any Facebook marketing email and click Unsubscribe at the bottom. Marketing mail is controlled separately from notifications.

If you also run a Facebook Page or ad account, those have their own notification settings, so check them too. For other senders that keep coming back, see How to Unsubscribe from Emails in Gmail.

Can Gmail automatically delete Facebook emails?

Yes. If you still want some Facebook alerts but don't want them piling up, a filter can clear them on a delay. In Gmail's search bar, type from:facebookmail.com -from:security@facebookmail.com older_than:7d, click the search options icon, choose Create filter, and tick Delete it. Also tick Also apply filter to matching conversations to clear the existing backlog at the same time.

New alerts stay in your inbox for a week, long enough to reply to an event invite or accept a friend request. After that, Gmail clears them. For more filter recipes and how to avoid deleting the wrong thing, see Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Will deleting Facebook emails in Gmail delete anything on my Facebook account?

No. Facebook notification emails are copies of alerts. Your posts, messages, friends, and notifications stay on Facebook, so deleting the emails in Gmail changes nothing on your account.

What email address does Facebook send notifications from?

Most Facebook notifications come from addresses ending in facebookmail.com, such as notification@facebookmail.com, friends@facebookmail.com, and groupupdates@facebookmail.com. Marketing and some account mail can also come from facebook.com or meta.com.

Should I delete Facebook security emails?

Old login codes and password reset emails are safe to delete. Keep recent security alerts until you have checked them, because they are how Facebook warns you about a sign-in you did not make.

Why am I getting Facebook emails when I don't use Facebook?

Inactive accounts get extra emails, like "you have new notifications" reminders designed to bring you back. Turn off email notifications in Facebook's settings, or deactivate or delete the account to stop them entirely.

Can I get deleted Facebook emails back?

Yes, for 30 days. Deleted emails sit in Gmail's Trash until Gmail removes them permanently after 30 days, or until you empty Trash yourself. Select them in Trash and click Move to Inbox to restore them.
