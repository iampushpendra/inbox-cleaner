Gmail Filters to Delete Emails Automatically

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: Open Gmail's search bar, click the sliders icon to set your criteria (sender, subject, or an operator like older_than:30d), click "Create filter," then check "Delete it" and save. New matching emails will skip your inbox and go straight to Trash. Filters don't touch mail that already arrived — for that backlog, Inbox Cleaner bulk-deletes by sender in one click.

Manually deleting the same junk mail every week gets old fast. If a sender reliably sends you things you'll never read — daily deal blasts, automated reports, notification digests — Gmail can delete them for you the moment they land, with zero ongoing effort. This is done through filters, one of Gmail's oldest and most underused features.

How do I create a Gmail filter to delete emails automatically?

  1. Click the sliders icon (search options) at the right edge of Gmail's search bar.
  2. Fill in what identifies the emails you want gone — a From address, a Subject keyword, or a search operator such as older_than:30d.
  3. Click Create filter at the bottom of the panel.
  4. Check the box next to Delete it in the list of actions.
  5. Click Create filter again to save it.

From that point on, any new email matching your criteria skips the inbox entirely and lands in Trash, where it still sits for 30 days before permanent removal — filters don't bypass that recovery window, they just automate the first step.

What are the best filter criteria for auto-deleting junk mail?

The filter is only as good as what you tell it to match. A few reliable patterns:

  • By sender: from:noreply@example.com — good for automated reports or digests from a single service.
  • By multiple senders: from:(deals@store.com OR offers@store.com) — groups related addresses from the same company into one rule.
  • By category: category:promotions older_than:30d — auto-clears marketing mail that's sat unread for a month. This is the same approach covered in how to delete all promotional emails in Gmail, applied as a standing rule instead of a one-time cleanup.
  • By subject keyword: subject:("weekly digest") — targets recurring automated emails with a consistent subject line.
  • By size: larger:10M — catches oversized attachments before they eat into your storage quota.

Test the search first by typing it into Gmail's search bar and pressing Enter before turning it into a filter. If the results look right, you know the filter will match correctly too.

Will a filter delete emails that are already in my inbox?

Not unless you tell it to. When creating a filter, Gmail shows a checkbox labeled "Also apply filter to matching conversations." Checking it applies the delete action retroactively to everything currently in your inbox that matches — but on a backlog of thousands of emails from one sender, this can be slow and sometimes silently stops partway through, the same limitation you'd hit trying to bulk delete Gmail emails manually.

How do I manage or edit filters I've already created?

  1. Open Gmail Settings (gear icon) and go to Filters and Blocked Addresses.
  2. Find the filter in the list — each one shows its search criteria and the actions it takes.
  3. Click edit to change its criteria or actions, or delete to remove it entirely.

It's worth reviewing this list every few months. Filters silently deleting mail from a sender you've since started caring about is an easy thing to forget about until you notice something's missing.

Frequently asked questions

Do Gmail filters delete emails that already arrived?

No, by default. A filter created from scratch only applies to mail that arrives after the filter is saved. To apply it retroactively, check "Also apply filter to matching conversations" when creating it — though this still runs into the same page limits as manual bulk delete for very large matches.

Can I filter by multiple senders in one Gmail filter?

Yes. Use "from:(sender1@example.com OR sender2@example.com OR sender3@example.com)" in the From field, or search "category:promotions" style operators to match a whole group at once instead of listing individual addresses.

Why isn't my Gmail delete filter working?

The most common cause is a filter that matches too narrowly — check the exact From address or subject text against a real example email. Also confirm "Delete it" is actually checked in the filter's action list, since it's easy to save a filter with only a label applied and no delete action.

Can Gmail filters delete emails older than a certain date automatically?

Yes, using the older_than operator, for example "older_than:1y" to target anything past one year old. Combine it with a sender or category, like "category:promotions older_than:30d", so the filter only auto-deletes aging mail in that specific group rather than everything.

How many filters can I create in Gmail?

Gmail allows up to 1,000 filters per account, which is far more than most people need. If you're creating a new filter for every single sender, it's usually faster to bulk-delete the backlog once and unsubscribe from repeat offenders instead.
