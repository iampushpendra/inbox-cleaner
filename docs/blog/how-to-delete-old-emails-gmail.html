How to Delete Old Emails in Gmail

Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: To delete old emails in Gmail, search older_than:1y (swap in 6m, 2y, or any period you want), click the checkbox above the results, then "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit delete. For inboxes with years of buildup across hundreds of senders, Inbox Cleaner ranks senders by volume so you can clear the biggest sources of old mail in one click.

Old emails don't just sit there quietly. They eat into your 15 GB storage quota, slow down search, and make it harder to find anything recent. Most of what's sitting in a five-year-old inbox is newsletters, notifications, and receipts nobody's opened in years — safe to clear out with the right search.

The tricky part isn't deleting one old email — it's deleting thousands of them without spending an afternoon scrolling. Gmail gives you a search operator built for exactly this, plus a bulk-select tool that extends past what's rendered on screen. Below is the manual method, followed by a faster route for inboxes with years of unsorted buildup across dozens of senders.

How do I delete old emails in Gmail?

Gmail's older_than: search operator filters your inbox to messages past a certain age, so you don't have to scroll back manually:

  1. Open Gmail and click the search bar.
  2. Type older_than:1y and press Enter — this shows every email over a year old. Swap 1y for 6m, 30d, or 2y depending on how far back you want to cut.
  3. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the message list to select everything on the page.
  4. A banner appears: "Select all conversations that match this search." Click it to extend the selection to every matching email, not just the current page.
  5. Click the Trash icon. Everything selected moves to Trash.

This works well for a single sweep. But Gmail's select-all banner can time out on very large result sets — the same limit you'd run into trying to bulk delete Gmail emails any other way — so a five-figure backlog sometimes needs a few passes.

How do I delete old emails from specific senders instead of by date?

Date alone doesn't tell you which old emails are safe to clear. A sender-first approach is usually faster: find who's actually filled your inbox with years of mail, then clear all of it from that sender at once. How do I stop old emails from piling up again?

A one-time cleanup only resets the clock. To keep old mail from stacking back up, set a Gmail filter that auto-deletes messages past a certain age from sources you don't need to keep — see Gmail filters to delete emails automatically for the exact setup with the older_than: operator baked into a filter's search criteria.

Should I delete old emails or archive them instead?

It depends on whether you might need to find the email again. Archiving removes a message from your inbox view but keeps it searchable forever — useful for old receipts or confirmations you might reference later. Deleting is better for anything with genuinely no future value: expired promotions, old notifications, or mail from services you no longer use. For the full breakdown of when each makes sense, see Gmail archive vs delete.

One thing worth remembering either way: archived emails still count toward your 15 GB storage limit. If storage is the actual problem, archiving won't fix it — only deleting (and then emptying Trash) recovers space. Deleted mail sits in Trash for 30 days before Gmail removes it for good, so a bulk cleanup is reversible right up until that window closes — you're not one wrong click away from losing something permanently.

Frequently asked questions

What does the older_than search operator accept in Gmail?

older_than: accepts a number followed by d (days), m (months), or y (years) — for example older_than:6m or older_than:2y. Gmail treats a month as 30 days, so older_than:1m is roughly the same as older_than:30d.

Will deleting old emails delete them from all my devices?

Yes. Gmail syncs across web, mobile, and any mail client connected via IMAP, so once an email moves to Trash it disappears from the inbox view everywhere within a minute or two.

Can I recover old emails after deleting them?

Yes, for 30 days. Deleted mail sits in Trash and can be dragged back to the inbox or any label during that window. After 30 days, or if you empty Trash manually, Gmail removes it permanently.

Should I delete old emails or just archive them?

Archive if you might need to search for something later but don't want it cluttering your inbox — archived mail still counts toward storage. Delete if you're trying to free up space or the content genuinely has no future value, like expired promotions or old notifications.

How old do emails need to be before it's safe to delete them?

There's no universal rule, but a common approach is one year for personal mail and 7 years for anything with tax or legal relevance. For newsletters, notifications, and promotions, 30-90 days is usually plenty since they have no lasting value.
