How to Delete All Emails from One Sender in Gmail

Updated June 2026 · 4 min read

If one sender has filled your inbox with hundreds (or thousands) of emails, there are two ways to delete them all: Gmail's built-in search method, and a faster tool that does it in one click. This guide covers both.

Method 1: Gmail's built-in search (free, no install)

Gmail lets you search by sender and then delete. Here's the exact process:

  1. Open Gmail in your browser and click the search bar.
  2. Type from:sender@example.com and press Enter. Replace the email address with the sender you want to delete.
  3. Gmail shows all emails from that sender. Click the checkbox in the top-left to select all emails on the current page (up to 50).
  4. A banner appears: "All 50 conversations on this page are selected." Click "Select all [X] conversations that match this search" to select everything, not just the current page.
  5. Click the trash icon to move them all to Trash.

That's it — but there's a catch.

The problem: Gmail caps at 50 per page

Step 4 above — selecting all matching emails — only works reliably for smaller batches. For large inboxes (thousands of emails from one sender), Gmail can time out or only partially complete the deletion. Many users report that Gmail's "select all" only processes the first page even after clicking the banner.

The workaround is to repeat the process: search, select all, delete, repeat. For a sender with 3,000 emails, that's 60 rounds of manual clicking.

Method 2: Inbox Cleaner (one click, no install)

Inbox Cleaner is a free browser tool that scans your entire Gmail inbox and ranks every sender by email count. You can select one or more senders and move all their emails to Trash in a single click — no paging, no repetition.

  1. Go to inbox-cleaner.vercel.app and click Connect Gmail.
  2. The tool scans your inbox (about 30 seconds for 5,000 emails) and ranks every sender by count — largest offenders first.
  3. Find the sender you want to delete. Check the box next to their name.
  4. Click Move to Trash. All their emails move at once.

You can select multiple senders at once and delete them all in one operation. The tool only reads From and Date headers — never email bodies — and everything runs in your browser.

Which method should you use?

Use Gmail's built-in search if you have fewer than 200 emails from a sender and don't mind a few extra clicks. Use Inbox Cleaner if you want to clean up multiple senders at once, or if you have thousands of emails to delete and don't want to repeat the process manually.

Will deleted emails be gone permanently?

No. Both methods move emails to Trash, not permanent deletion. Gmail keeps Trash for 30 days. If you accidentally delete something important, you can restore it from Trash within that window. To permanently delete, you'd need to empty Trash manually.

Can I delete emails from a sender on mobile?

The Gmail app on iOS and Android doesn't support selecting all emails from a search in one step — you can only select individual emails. For bulk deletion on mobile, the Gmail web app in a mobile browser works better. Inbox Cleaner also works on mobile browsers, though the desktop experience is easier for reviewing the sender list.

What if I don't know the sender's exact email address?

In Gmail's search bar, you can search by partial email or display name: from:newsletter will match any sender whose email contains "newsletter". Inbox Cleaner shows you the full display name and email for every sender, so you can spot bulk senders without knowing their exact address upfront.
