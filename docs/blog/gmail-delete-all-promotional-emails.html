How to Delete All Promotional Emails in Gmail

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Open Gmail's Promotions tab, click the top checkbox, then "Select all conversations that match this search," and hit delete. This handles a page at a time reliably; for a Promotions tab with thousands of emails built up over years, Inbox Cleaner clears it in one click with no page-by-page limit.

Gmail automatically routes marketing emails, deals, and newsletters into a separate Promotions tab so they don't clutter your primary inbox. That's useful for reading — less useful when you actually want to clear the tab out, because Gmail's bulk-select tools still cap out the same way they do everywhere else.

How do I delete all promotional emails in Gmail?

  1. Open Gmail and click the Promotions tab above your inbox.
  2. Click the checkbox at the top-left of the message list to select everything visible.
  3. A banner appears: "Select all conversations that match this search." Click it to extend the selection to the entire tab, not just the current page.
  4. Click the Trash icon. Everything selected moves to Trash.

This handles most Promotions tabs cleanly. But on very large tabs — tens of thousands of emails built up over years — Gmail's select-all link can time out or fail partway through, the same problem you'd hit trying to bulk delete Gmail emails from anywhere else in your inbox. When that happens, you're back to deleting 50 at a time.

How do I stop promotional emails from piling up again?

Deleting the backlog once doesn't stop new promotional emails from arriving. Two things help:

First, unsubscribe from senders you never actually read — most promotional emails have an unsubscribe link at the bottom, and Gmail also surfaces an "Unsubscribe" button next to the sender name for many of them.

Second, set up a filter to auto-delete what you don't unsubscribe from. In Gmail's search bar, type category:promotions older_than:30d, click the dropdown arrow, choose Create filter, then check Delete it. Anything landing in Promotions will auto-clear after 30 days without you touching it.

Will this affect emails I actually want to keep?

Gmail's categorization is usually accurate — receipts, shipping confirmations, and account notifications typically land in your primary inbox, not Promotions, because they're transactional rather than marketing. Still, skim the first page of your Promotions tab before a mass delete if you're not sure what's landed there; a quick scan takes under a minute and confirms nothing important slipped in.

If you're clearing out Promotions as part of a bigger cleanup, it pairs well with getting to Gmail inbox zero — Promotions is usually the single biggest category once you start ranking senders by volume.

Frequently asked questions

Does deleting the Promotions tab delete everything in it permanently?

No. Deleting moves emails to Trash, where they sit for 30 days before Gmail permanently removes them. You can restore anything from Trash during that window.

Why do promotional emails keep coming back after I unsubscribe?

Unsubscribing only stops future emails from that specific sender — it doesn't affect emails already in your inbox, and it doesn't stop other senders. Expect a mix of manual unsubscribing and periodic bulk cleanup.

Is it safe to delete all Promotions tab emails at once?

Yes, for most people. Gmail's Promotions tab is machine-sorted and rarely contains anything you'd need later — order confirmations and receipts usually land in the primary inbox, not Promotions. Skim the first page before bulk-deleting if you're unsure.

Can I automatically delete promotional emails in Gmail going forward?

Yes. Create a Gmail filter with the search 'category:promotions older_than:30d', then choose 'Delete it' as the action. New promotional emails will auto-delete after 30 days without any manual cleanup.

What's the difference between Promotions tab emails and Spam?

Promotions are legitimate marketing emails from senders you interacted with or signed up to — Gmail just deprioritizes them. Spam is unsolicited or malicious mail Gmail has flagged as unwanted. They're cleaned up separately.
