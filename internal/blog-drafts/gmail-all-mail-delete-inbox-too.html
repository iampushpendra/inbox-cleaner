Does Deleting an Email From All Mail Also Delete It From the Inbox?

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Yes. In Gmail on the web, deleting an email from All Mail also removes it from your inbox and from every label, because All Mail, Inbox and your labels are views of the same single message, not separate copies. The message moves to Trash, where you can restore it for up to 30 days. If you only want it out of your inbox, use Archive instead of Delete.

Gmail doesn't file mail into folders. It puts labels on one stored message. Inbox is one of those labels, and All Mail shows your mail whether or not it still has the Inbox label. Gmail's labels help says labels are different from folders and that when you delete an email, "it's removed from every label and your inbox." So it doesn't matter which view you delete from. The result is the same.

How do I remove an email from All Mail without losing it?

You can't. All Mail is where Gmail shows every message you still have. The only way to make a message leave All Mail is to delete it. If you want a calmer view instead, archive mail out of the inbox, add labels, or use search. Gmail archive vs. delete helps you choose between keeping and removing a message.

How do I delete from All Mail safely?

  1. Decide what you want gone. If you only want the message out of your inbox, use Archive. Delete takes it out of every label too.
  2. Check the labels on the message. Open it and look at the labels next to the subject. Each of those views will lose the message.
  3. Check the conversation. With conversation view on, the list checkbox selects a whole conversation. To delete just one message, open it, click More next to Reply, then Delete this message.
  4. Delete and confirm in Trash. Click Delete. The message moves to Trash. You can move it back for up to 30 days unless you delete it forever or empty Trash sooner.

Be careful with select-all in All Mail. The select box covers the current page, and the banner above the list extends it to every conversation in All Mail. That includes archived mail you never see in the inbox. How to select all emails in Gmail explains the page-versus-all difference before you click Delete.

If you restore a message from Trash, Google says you can move it back to your inbox. The help page doesn't say whether every original label comes back, so check the labels after restoring anything important.

Why does deleting in Outlook, Apple Mail or Thunderbird work differently?

Email apps that connect to Gmail through IMAP see labels as folders. Google's IMAP client settings help says that "you can find messages you delete from an IMAP folder in your 'All Mail' label in Gmail." In practice, deleting from the inbox folder in one of those apps can act like archiving: the inbox label goes, and the message stays in All Mail. Exactly what happens depends on the app and its settings. If you want a message in Trash, delete it in Gmail on the web. This guide covers Gmail on a computer; the Gmail mobile apps are not covered here.

Yes. Removing everything from one sender reaches the same single copy, so it clears that mail from All Mail, the inbox and every label at once. Gmail's own method is a from: search plus the select-all banner. How to delete all emails from one sender in Gmail walks through it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I delete an email from All Mail but keep it in my inbox?

No. All Mail and the inbox show the same message, not two copies. Gmail's labels help says deleting an email removes it from every label and your inbox. If you only want a tidier view, use search or labels instead of deleting.

If I delete an email from a label, is it deleted everywhere?

Yes, in Gmail on the web. Clicking Delete while viewing a label moves the message to Trash, which removes it from the inbox, All Mail and its other labels. To take it out of that one label only, uncheck the label in the Labels menu instead.

Why is an email I deleted in Outlook or Apple Mail still in Gmail's All Mail?

Email apps that connect to Gmail through IMAP treat Gmail labels as folders. Google's IMAP settings help says messages you delete from an IMAP folder can be found in All Mail, so the app removed a label rather than sending the message to Trash. Delete it in Gmail on the web if you want it in Trash.
