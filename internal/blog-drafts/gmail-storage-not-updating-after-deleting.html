Why Is Gmail Storage Still Full After Deleting Emails?

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Gmail storage often stays full after you delete emails because deleting only moves them to Trash, and Trash still counts toward your Google storage for up to 30 days. If Trash is clear, check whether you deleted in another account, whether an IMAP mail app archived the messages instead of deleting them, and whether Drive or Photos uses most of your space. Google says totals normally take up to 48–72 hours to update after a large deletion.

Your Google Account storage is shared by Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos. Google's storage help says Gmail storage includes messages and attachments in the Spam and Trash folders. Deleting a message changes which folder it is in. It doesn't remove it from your storage.

Where did the emails I deleted actually go?

In Gmail itself, deleted messages go to Trash. Gmail's deletion help says you can restore them for up to 30 days. After that they are deleted permanently. Until then they still take up space.

Messages deleted in another email app may not go to Trash at all. Google's recommended IMAP client settings say you can find messages you delete from an IMAP folder in your All Mail label in Gmail. So deleting in Outlook, Apple Mail or Thunderbird can leave the message in Gmail, where it still uses storage. Archiving works the same way. The guide to Gmail Archive versus Delete explains the difference.

How do I check each cause in order?

  1. Confirm the account. Check that the Google Account showing full storage is the same account you deleted mail from.
  2. Look in Trash. In Gmail on a computer, search in:trash. If the deleted messages are there, they still count. Also search in:spam.
  3. Check mail deleted in another app. If you deleted in an IMAP client, search Gmail on the web for one of those messages. If it's there without Trash, the app archived it rather than deleting it.
  4. Read the storage breakdown. Open the account's Google storage page and see which service uses the most space. If Drive or Photos dominates, deleting more email won't help much.
  5. Check message sizes. Search larger:10M to find large messages that remain. Look at the results before deleting. This is a size threshold, not a guarantee of how much space you'll get back.
  6. Allow time for the update. Google says totals normally take up to 48–72 hours to update after a large deletion. Recheck then, instead of deleting more mail because the number hasn't moved.

Should I empty Trash right away?

You can, but treat it as a permanent decision. Gmail's help says you can delete all messages in Trash with Empty Trash now. After that you can't recover them through Gmail. Before you empty it, check Trash for receipts, records and other mail you still need. The guide to deleting Gmail emails permanently walks through that step.

Will a sender cleanup tool lower my storage?

Not on its own. Inbox Cleaner groups Gmail mail by sender in desktop Chrome, with Gmail open in a tab, and moves the senders you choose to Trash. Like ordinary Gmail deletion, that mail keeps counting toward storage until Trash is emptied or the 30-day period ends. The extension shows message counts, not storage size. It also can't limit a sender-wide cleanup by date, size, category or starred status. For storage problems, a size-based Gmail search is usually a better place to start. Use sender cleanup when a few senders fill your inbox with mail you don't need. See how to delete all emails from one sender in Gmail for the native method.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Gmail storage take to update after deleting emails?

Google says that after a large number of deletions it normally takes up to 48–72 hours for storage totals to update accurately. That applies to items that are actually gone. Mail still in Trash keeps counting until it is removed from Trash.

Does Spam count toward Gmail storage?

Yes. Google's storage help says Gmail storage includes messages and attachments in the Spam and Trash folders.

Does archiving emails free up Gmail storage?

No. Archived mail stays in All Mail and still uses storage. Only mail that has left Trash stops counting.
