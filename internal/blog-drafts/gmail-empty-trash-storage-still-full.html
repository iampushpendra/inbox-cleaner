Why Is Gmail Storage Still Full After Emptying Trash?

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Gmail storage can stay full after you empty Trash because Gmail's Trash is only one of several places that count. Spam, Google Drive trash, Google Photos trash, Drive files, photos and backups share the same Google Account quota, and a family plan shares one quota across its members. Google says totals normally take up to 48–72 hours to update after a large deletion, so recheck the account's storage breakdown before deleting more mail.

Emptying Gmail's Trash permanently removes those messages. Gmail's help says you can't recover them through Gmail after that. If the storage number didn't drop, the space is usually being used somewhere else. Work through the checks below in order. Each check rules out one place.

Google's storage help lists what counts: Gmail messages and attachments, including Spam and Trash; Drive files; photos and videos backed up in Original quality, and Storage saver items backed up after 1 June 2021; Android backups; WhatsApp backups on Android. That's why emptying Gmail's Trash alone can leave the total high. If you also can't receive mail, what to check when your Gmail mailbox is full covers delivery.

How do I check each cause in order?

  1. Confirm the account. Make sure the Google Account whose storage is full is the same account whose Gmail Trash you emptied. This matters most when you're signed in to several accounts in one browser.
  2. Check Spam. In Gmail on a computer, search in:spam. Empty Trash now clears Trash only, and Google counts Spam toward storage.
  3. Check Drive and Photos trash. Each app has its own trash. Google's Drive help says files in Trash count toward storage until they're permanently deleted. Google's storage help lists Gmail, Drive and Photos trash together as items that count until cleared. Both Drive and Photos delete trashed items forever after 30 days. Emptying either one is permanent.
  4. Read the usage breakdown. Open the Google storage page for the account and look at usage by service. If Drive, Photos or backups use most of the space, deleting more email won't help much.
  5. Check a family plan. Google's family sharing help says members of a Google One family group share the family group's storage quota. The same page explains how to check storage usage by family member, and says members can only free up space from their own storage usage.
  6. Allow time for the update. Google says storage normally takes up to 48–72 hours to update accurately after a large deletion. Recheck then, instead of deleting more because the number hasn't moved.

The in:spam and larger: operators are documented in Google's Gmail search operator reference. A size search shows large messages, not how much space you'll get back.

How long should I wait before deleting more?

Google gives two timings. Google's Drive storage help says space updates after a short period. It suggests advanced cleanup if the account is still full after 30 minutes. The same page says to allow up to 48–72 hours after deleting many files at once. Google's storage FAQ also says large deletions normally take up to 48–72 hours.

A practical reading: check after 30 minutes for small cleanups. After a large one, don't judge the result until 48–72 hours have passed. Neither figure is a guarantee, and this page doesn't give a countdown.

What if Gmail really is using the space?

Then the mail is still in your mailbox: in the inbox, under labels or archived in All Mail. Archiving doesn't free space; see Gmail archive vs. delete. Frequently asked questions

Does Empty Trash now in Gmail also empty Drive or Photos trash?

No. Empty Trash now clears Gmail's Trash only. Google Drive and Google Photos each have their own trash, and Google says items in those trash folders count toward storage until they are permanently deleted.

Can I get emails back after emptying Gmail Trash?

Not through Gmail. Gmail's help says messages in Trash can be recovered for up to 30 days; once Trash is emptied, they are deleted permanently.

Why does Google give different times for storage to update?

Google's Drive storage help says to recheck after 30 minutes and also to allow up to 48–72 hours after deleting multiple files. Google's storage FAQ says large deletions normally take up to 48–72 hours. Treat 30 minutes as an early check, not a deadline.
