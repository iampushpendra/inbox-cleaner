Gmail Advanced Search Operators Cheat Sheet for Cleanup

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read · All guides

Quick answer: The Gmail advanced search operators that matter most for cleanup are from:, list:, category:, older_than:, before:, larger:, has:attachment and is:starred, joined with spaces (AND), OR, { }, ( ) and -. Type them in the Gmail search box on a computer. Before you delete, remember three limits Google documents: Gmail matches messages and can return whole mixed conversations, standard search skips Spam and Trash, and from: also matches a person's aliases.

Google's Refine searches in Gmail page is the authoritative operator list, and most cheat sheets copy it as operator, description and example. This one is arranged around cleanup: which operator does each job, and what it can catch or miss before you click Delete. Everything below was checked against Google's help pages on October 22, 2026. It is a documentation review, not a live mailbox test. For age searches in depth, see Gmail search operators for reviewing old emails.

How do I combine Gmail search operators correctly?

SyntaxMeaningExample
Space or ANDAll conditions must matchfrom:deals@example.com older_than:1y
OR or { }Any condition matches{from:a@example.com from:b@example.com}
( )Groups termssubject:(sale clearance)
-Excludes a termcategory:promotions -from:bank@example.com

Google's reference does not say how OR and a plain space combine when you use both in one search. A search such as from:a@example.com OR from:b@example.com older_than:1y could be read two ways, and the difference decides whether recent mail from one sender is included. Remove the doubt with braces: {from:a@example.com from:b@example.com} older_than:1y. Write OR in capitals, as Google's examples do. Google also notes that in search terms a space or dash separates numbers and a dot is a decimal point.

Which searches can catch mail I meant to keep?

Gmail identifies matching messages first, then shows their conversations. Google gives the key example: searching -is:starred can return an entire conversation if it contains at least one unstarred message, even when other messages in that conversation are starred. The same logic applies when you combine an age limit with a sender: an old conversation can include a recent reply. Selecting and deleting the conversation from the results list affects the whole conversation, not only the message that matched.

Three other gaps are worth checking. An address search can include aliases, so quote it when you want only one address. Labels sit on messages, so has:nouserlabels can return a conversation where one reply is labelled. And because standard search skips Spam and Trash, a clean result does not mean those folders are empty.

How do I build a cleanup search step by step?

Add one condition at a time and look at the results after each. This is a procedure for your own mailbox; the example sender is a placeholder.

  1. Start with who. Search "from:deals@example.com" and open a few results to confirm they are the mail you mean.
  2. Add when. Add older_than:1y. Rerun and note how many conversations remain.
  3. Add exclusions. Add -is:starred -is:important, keeping in mind that a returned conversation can still contain starred or important messages.
  4. Review mixed conversations. Switch results to Most recent and open conversations with several messages. Handle anything you want to keep separately.
  5. Delete and recheck. Select the page, use the select-all-conversations link if Gmail offers it, click Delete, then rerun the search. Google says deleted messages stay in Trash for 30 days unless you remove them sooner.

The selection step is covered in detail in how to select all emails in Gmail. If the search is one you will repeat, Google lets you open the search options panel, click Create filter and choose an action; see Gmail filters to delete emails automatically.

When is a sender list simpler than a search query?

Operators are the right tool when the cleanup depends on age, category, size, labels or starred status. If the job is simply "remove everything from these senders", writing and checking a from: query for each one is repetitive. Inbox Cleaner, which publishes this guide, runs in an open Gmail tab in desktop Chrome, lists senders by how many conversations Gmail shows for them, and moves the selected senders' mail to Trash. Its sender-wide action does not apply date, category, size or starred limits, includes archived mail from that sender, and its counts are conversation counts rather than storage. When you need any of those limits, use native Gmail search. For the manual version, see how to delete all emails from one sender in Gmail.

Frequently asked questions

Does Gmail search include Spam and Trash?

Not by default. Google says a standard search leaves out Spam and Trash. Use in:anywhere, or choose Mail & Spam & Trash in the search options, when you need them.

Can I sort Gmail search results by size?

Google documents two sort orders for search results: Most relevant and Most recent. To find large mail, filter with larger: and lower the threshold step by step.

Can I turn a cleanup search into a filter?

Yes. In the search options panel, enter the criteria, click Search to check the results, then click Create filter and choose an action such as Delete. Check the results first, because the filter acts on mail you will not see.

Do I need to remember these operators?

No. The search options panel and the search chips cover From, To, Subject, words, Size, dates and attachments. Operators matter when you need something the panel lacks, such as category:, list:, older_than: or a grouped OR.
