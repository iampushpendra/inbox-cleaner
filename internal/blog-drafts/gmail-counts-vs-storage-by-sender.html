Does the Sender With the Most Gmail Emails Use the Most Storage?

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: The sender with the most Gmail emails does not necessarily use the most storage. Storage depends on how big each message is, so a few emails with large attachments can outweigh thousands of small notifications. To compare senders, search Gmail on a computer for from: plus larger: and smaller: size bands. Gmail filters by size but does not report a storage total per sender.

Gmail counts toward your Google storage based on its messages and attachments, including mail in Spam and Trash (Google One storage help). That's bytes, not messages. A count tells you who is filling your inbox. It doesn't tell you who is filling your quota.

Why can a smaller sender use more storage?

In this example, deleting the noisiest sender clears 3,000 messages but frees less space than 40 scanner emails would. One 10 MB message is about the same as 500 messages of 20 KB. The order flips because of message size. Message count barely matters.

The reverse can also happen. A high-volume sender that attaches invoices or images can lead in both rankings. You can't tell which case you have without checking sizes.

How do I check how much storage one sender uses?

Gmail's search reference lists from: for a sender and size:, larger: and smaller: for message size, with larger:10M as an example (Gmail search operators). Combine them to sort one sender's mail into size bands:

  1. In Gmail on a computer, search from:sender@example.com larger:5M and note how many results appear.
  2. Search from:sender@example.com larger:1M smaller:5M and note the count.
  3. Search from:sender@example.com smaller:1M and note the count.
  4. Repeat for the other senders you're considering. Compare their large bands first. A sender with mail in the larger bands usually outweighs a sender whose mail is all in the small band, even if that second sender has far more messages.
  5. Before deleting anything, check your storage breakdown by service. Gmail, Drive and Photos share the same storage, and Google's storage help shows where to find usage per service.

Each band gives a rough range, not an exact figure. Five results in the 1–5 MB band means somewhere between about 5 MB and 25 MB. The small band has a weak upper limit, but it rarely decides the comparison.

What can this method miss?

  • Results are conversations. Gmail identifies matching messages first and can then show a whole conversation (Google's search caveat). A conversation can contain replies of other sizes, or messages from other people.
  • Units aren't fully documented. Google's page gives size:1000000 and larger:10M as examples but doesn't define the exact unit sizes. Treat the bands as approximate.
  • One sender, several addresses. A company may send from several addresses. from: only matches what you type, so check each address.
  • Trash and Spam still count. Mail you've already deleted sits in Trash for up to 30 days and keeps using storage until it's permanently removed (Gmail delete and recover help).

When does clearing a high-count sender still make sense?

Sometimes clutter is the real problem rather than storage. Removing a sender's thousands of notifications makes the inbox easier to scan, even if it frees little space. For that job, see how to delete all emails from one sender in Gmail. To select a large result set by hand, read how to select all emails in Gmail.

Frequently asked questions

Can Gmail show how much storage each sender uses?

Not as a report. Gmail search can filter one sender's mail by size with larger: and smaller:, which lets you estimate a range, but it does not total bytes per sender.

Will deleting a high-volume sender free up a lot of space?

Only if that sender's messages are also large. Thousands of small notification emails can add up to less than a handful of messages with big attachments. Moved mail also keeps counting while it sits in Trash.

Does Inbox Cleaner show storage per sender?

No. It ranks senders by email count, and those counts are not storage measurements. Use Gmail size searches to check bytes before deciding.
