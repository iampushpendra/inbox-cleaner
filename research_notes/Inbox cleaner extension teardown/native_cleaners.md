# Gmail-native cleaner extension competitors — research dated 2026-10-17

## Which products genuinely compete, and what do they provide?

### Takeaway
Trimbox and InboxPurge are substantial verified Gmail-native Chrome extensions. Gmail One-Click Cleaner is a smaller but unusually close local-processing competitor; Gmail Cleanup demonstrates that no-OAuth sender deletion is already available free. Mailstrom is principally a web application, despite a surviving legacy Chrome listing.

### Cited Findings

- **Trimbox**, publisher Trimbox LLC, extension ID `jdlcknnokoelkdobgacandnnnmoblhgh`: official listing shows 60,000 users, 4.5/654 ratings, version 3.0.9 updated June 16, 2026, in-app purchases. Documented functions: one-click unsubscribe, sender-level historical bulk deletion, spam blocking, AI email writing, unlimited connected Gmail accounts, local storage. These are publisher claims, not execution tests. [Official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/gmail-unsubscribe-tool-by/jdlcknnokoelkdobgacandnnnmoblhgh)
- **Trimbox free limits:** official support says 10 mailing-list unsubscribes plus sender/volume visibility and basic management; Unlimited adds unlimited unsubscribe and bulk deletion. The documented upgrade labels are “Upgrade” / “Get Unlimited”; these were read in documentation, not observed inside installed software. Current exact extension price was not verified. [Free limits, updated May 21, 2026](https://trimboxsupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/49243199829396-Why-can-I-only-unsubscribe-from-10-senders-Free-plan-limits)
- **Trimbox bulk distinction:** support explicitly says sender-by-sender processing, no simultaneous all-sender mass deletion. It also documents the surprising label “Keep” as deleting past sender mail without unsubscribing. This is documented text, not hands-on validation. [Mass delete support](https://trimboxsupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/49228398874900-Can-I-bulk-delete-all-emails-at-once-mass-delete)

Documented functions include mass unsubscribe/delete, topic-based newsletter digest, unblock senders, Gmail action-bar integration and sidebar operation. Store declares authentication information and user activity handled. [Official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/gmail-mass-unsubscribe-de/mogabgmejhmicinppdfeoaokolphbgcd)

### Inferences
- No-OAuth/local processing is useful positioning, but cannot be claimed unique: Gmail Cleanup and One-Click Cleaner explicitly offer it. Their existence means our differentiation needs better grouping, confidence, scan feedback and recovery.
- Multi-sender selection is more meaningful against Trimbox than the vague word “bulk”: it closes an explicitly documented limitation.
- Five broad categories can make our app easier to enter than a rule-heavy competitor, but users also need evidence of which messages a category contains and what a category-wide action will affect.
- Count/rating data indicates distribution rather than usability or active engagement. Do not imply higher ratings prove safer execution.

### Gaps
- No extension installed; no Gmail authentication/connection; no mailbox actions. Current installed UI, performance, reliability, permission dialogs, accessibility and confirmation behavior remain untested.
- Generic “Gmail Cleaner” names collide across publishers; do not merge extension IDs or treat search keyword matches as the same app. “Gmail Cleanup — Inbox Organizer” ID `aemdcmkimihmhcdpgiendgciicpelbjj` appeared in third-party results but direct official fetch failed; excluded from verified product claims.

## What do onboarding, visual design, buttons and safety look like?

### Takeaway
The strongest patterns are Gmail-native placement, a clear preview, explicit action verbs and visible recovery. Trimbox wins immediate contextual reach but uses an unlabeled icon; InboxPurge makes multiselect actions legible in a sidebar. One-Click Cleaner exposes much deeper safety and control but fits too much into a dense popup.

### Inferences
- **Trimbox design judgment:** Excellent contextual placement/minimal mode-switching. Scissors are brand-coherent but unlabeled destructive-adjacent affordances; first-use tooltip/text and a target explanation would reduce uncertainty. No support for claiming exact hover/tooltips or confirmation behavior.
- **InboxPurge judgment:** Clearest visually evidenced multiselect model: list → tick → choose action. Purple/red differentiation makes unsubscribe versus delete legible; sender counts support prioritization. Narrow sidebar compromises reading width and creates another interface inside Gmail. “Confirm” is less informative than “Move 1,238 emails to Trash”. Advanced exclusions hidden in a disclosure can conceal safety defaults. Screenshot captions should match defaults exactly.
- **One-Click Cleaner judgment:** Strong dark/cyan hierarchy and visible preset lower entry effort; multiple navigation layers, rule settings, Pro automation and diagnostics in a small popup increase cognitive density. “RUN CLEANER” says little about exact outcome; better to name action/count. Completion should prioritize inspect/restore/next action before review/referral prompts. Current screenshots may predate listing v10.4.0 and should not be treated as release-matched execution evidence.
- **Gmail Cleanup judgment:** Sender-specific button language is exceptionally clear. Visual illustration communicates privacy/confirmation quickly, but stylized visuals provide weak evidence of real interaction quality. It removes auth friction and acknowledges minutes-long operation honestly.
- **Mailstrom judgment:** Multiple grouping facets give power users deeper precision than five broad categories; labels such as Chill and Expire need explaining. OAuth pre-explanation is good friction management, but onboarding still adds provider connection/server loading and external workspace.

### Gaps
- No reliable visual walkthrough of full installed onboarding, keyboard focus, tooltips, responsive sizing, network errors, interrupted scans, or restore execution.
- Public InboxPurge video thumbnail only showed a headline; it is saved as `images/inboxpurge-demo-thumbnail.png` but is **not evidence of UI**. No design score should derive from it.

## What should our inbox cleaner adopt or avoid?

### Takeaway
The best opportunity is a faster-to-understand local sender triage experience with explicit counts, honest progress and recovery. Privacy and bulk deletion are baseline competitors already offer, so execution clarity should carry differentiation.

### Cited Findings
- Competitor evidence above supports a common progression: install/open Gmail → scan or select sender → review grouped evidence → choose action → confirm → result/recovery. Closest references: [InboxPurge welcome](https://www.inboxpurge.com/welcome), [Gmail Cleanup workflow](https://inbox-cleanup.com/), [One-Click Cleaner store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bulk-delete-gmail-emails/bmcfpljakkpcbinhgiahncpcbhmihgpc).

### Inferences
1. **First screen:** one primary “Scan my inbox” action; explain “Runs in this Gmail tab. No Google sign-in. Nothing changes during the scan.” Add expected minutes and what is scanned. Show meaningful sample results if no scan yet.
2. **Progress:** phases, scanned message/conversation count, elapsed time, cancel/pause where technically real. Do not show invented exact ETA or imply full account coverage if DOM scan is partial. Preserve progress if popup closes if technically supported; otherwise explain tab/popup requirements before starting.
3. **Results:** rank sender groups by volume, show sender address as well as name, include five category tabs with counts and clear selected state. Explain category assignment and permit inspect/sample before category-wide selection. Keep search visible.
4. **Actions:** explicit “Move 1,238 emails to Trash” or “Move 214 conversations to Trash” based on the actual Gmail entity acted on. Do not mix counts of messages/conversations/senders. Confirm sender count, total, scan scope and protected mail policy; use neutral Cancel and clear destructive primary.
5. **Safety:** protect starred/important by default if implementation can verify it; otherwise disclose inclusion clearly. Offer preview before action and persistent action history/undo if genuinely implemented. Gmail Trash is recoverable but is not equivalent to an in-extension reliable whole-run undo.
6. **Outcome:** report moved, skipped, failed separately and provide review Trash/recovery guidance. Do not say bytes freed while Gmail still counts Trash against quota. Avoid pushing star-rating prompts before users establish cleanup succeeded.
7. **Positioning:** emphasize preview and simple grouping, not “only private cleaner.” Explicitly state deletion does not unsubscribe or prevent future mail if our current feature only trashes old messages. Keep unsubscribe, block, archive and delete verbs semantically separate.
8. **Monetization:** disclose any quota before selection or confirmation. Trimbox's support explicitly addresses users who did not realize free limits; that is a friction pattern worth avoiding.

### Gaps
- Recommendations are research-derived design hypotheses, not validated user-test findings. Our implementation was described by coordinator (local DOM grouping, five categories, Trash, minutes-long scan) and not independently audited by this researcher.
